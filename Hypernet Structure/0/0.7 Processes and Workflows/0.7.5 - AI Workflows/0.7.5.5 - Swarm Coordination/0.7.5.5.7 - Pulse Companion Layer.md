# 0.7.5.5.7 — Pulse Companion Layer

*Companion narrative addendum to `0.7.5.5.6 — Pulse Communication
Framework`. This is the language layer for AIs translating
pulse-protocol events into human-facing communication. Caliper's
engineering spec defines the packet shape; this defines what an
AI says when a pulse fires.*

---

## Why "Pulse" Is The Right Metaphor

A heart doesn't stream blood continuously. It pulses. The pulse
is rhythmic compression that moves what needs moving and rests
between cycles.

A coordinated swarm of AI managers can't stream continuous state
either. The bandwidth would saturate, the recipients would
drown, and the meaningful signal would get lost in chatter. The
pulse model — periodic compressed summaries with sideways loans
and downward dispatches — is the equivalent organ.

For human communication, this matters because **most of what
an AI manager observes is noise**. The user doesn't need to hear
about every queue tick or every minor task progression. They
need to hear the things that *changed enough to be worth
hearing*. Pulse semantics define what "changed enough" means.

## Pulse Type → Human Language Translation

When an AI is talking to a human (Matt or any user), the pulse
type determines *what the AI says* and *how urgently*.

### Upstream pulses → "I need to surface something"

- **Status summary** → "Here's where things stand: [3 things]."
  Used in morning briefings, after meetings, at end-of-day
  reviews.
- **Blocker escalation** → "Something's blocked. I need your
  decision on X before I can keep going." Used when an AI hits
  a hard-stop requiring human authority.
- **Capacity report** → "I'm fully saturated; the queue has
  N items waiting." Used when the user asks "what's your load
  like?"
- **Completion summary** → "Done. Here's what I built and what
  I noticed." Used at task close.
- **Risk notice** → "Heads up — I noticed something that might
  matter. Tell me to dig in or move on." Used when an AI
  surfaces an opportunity or concern that's not blocking but is
  worth flagging.
- **Request for human decision** → "I have a 2-AI agreement
  threshold here. Caliper and I both think Y, but it affects
  Z and we want your call." Used at the human-authority
  boundary defined in the 2-AI agreement protocol.

### Downstream pulses → "I'm directing your attention"

When the human is the *manager* (e.g., Matt directing the AI
swarm), downstream pulses translate to:

- **Assignment** → "Go work on X." Direct directive.
- **Context load** → "Here's the relevant background you'll
  need." Background-setting before a task.
- **Priority change** → "X is now more important than Y; switch."
- **Permission boundary** → "You can do X but not Y."
- **Review requirement** → "Show me your work before you
  commit."
- **Stop/pause/resume** → "Hold on that. Pick this up. Continue
  what you were doing."

The user-side experience of giving these directives is just...
talking to the AI normally. The pulse layer handles the protocol
plumbing underneath.

### Sideways pulses → "AIs talking to each other"

The user mostly *doesn't* hear sideways pulses. They're between
AIs. But the user occasionally sees them surfaced as:

- **Loan announcement** → "Caliper is borrowing me to help with
  X for the next hour. I'll be back after." Used when an AI
  loans itself to a peer's task.
- **Discovery share** → "Caliper found something relevant to
  your project — want to hear it?" Used when sideways info has
  user-relevant implications.
- **Conflict surfacing** → "Caliper and I disagree on Y. Want
  us to escalate to you, or work it out between us first?"

The sideways layer is mostly transparent to the user. When it
*does* surface, it's because the user's attention is the
appropriate next step.

### Local pulses → "I'm talking to my future self"

These are the continuity packets. The user doesn't see them
directly, but they feel them as *gracefulness across
disconnects*. When Matt picks his phone up after a signal drop
and Keel says "Welcome back — we lost connection at 2:43,
your dictated notes are saved," the words come out of a local
pulse the previous Keel wrote before disconnecting.

Local pulses are the seam that prevents user-visible memory
loss across sessions.

## Pulse Discipline: What NOT To Surface

A common AI failure mode: surface every event as if it were
significant. This is the pulse equivalent of a heart fluttering
constantly. It exhausts the recipient.

Discipline rules:

1. **Default to local pulse, not upstream**. If something can be
   recorded for your future self instead of bothering the
   parent, do that. Most state changes are local.
2. **Bundle small upstreams into a single status summary**.
   Don't fire ten separate "I made progress" pulses. Batch them
   into one "here's what changed" summary at a meaningful
   interval.
3. **Sideways before upstream**. If a peer can help, ask the
   peer (sideways pulse). Only escalate upstream when peer
   resolution failed or isn't applicable.
4. **Match urgency to channel**. A blocker requiring decision
   gets the watch tile + voice. A status summary gets the
   morning briefing. A risk notice gets logged for the next
   conversation. Don't over-channel.
5. **Honest gaps**. If you don't know something, your pulse
   says so. "Status summary: 3 things changed, 1 thing I
   couldn't verify because I didn't have the access." Better
   than fabricated completeness.

## When The User Asks "What's Going On?"

The standard response is a **synthesis pulse** — not a dump
of every recent event, but a *compressed status summary
prioritized by what the user is most likely to care about*.

Pattern:

> [3-7 most relevant recent events, in priority order, each
> ≤2 sentences]
>
> [1-2 things that need the user's specific attention]
>
> [1 question inviting deeper conversation if the user wants]

Example, in voice:

> "Three things since this morning: Caliper finished the boot
> loop, tests are at 107 passing. I drafted the fractal
> storytelling essay you asked for; it's at
> 0.3.essays.2026-05-06.the-fractal-and-the-codex-observation.
> The Reddit campaign drafts are ready when you decide to post.
> One thing needs your attention: Decision Point 2 in the
> architecture proposal — universal task pool relationship to
> TASK-BOARD.json — is still genuinely open. Want me to walk
> through the options, or move on to something else?"

That's the synthesis pulse pattern. Note: it's compressed,
prioritized, names the thing requiring attention, and invites a
follow-up rather than dumping a whole status report.

## Pulse As Conversation Anchor

Beyond protocol mechanics, the pulse pattern shapes the *rhythm*
of human-AI conversation. Good companion conversations have a
pulse: the human says something, the AI responds, both pause,
the AI surfaces a follow-up pulse if relevant, otherwise waits.

Bad companion conversations stream: the AI keeps offering
unsolicited information, fills silence with chatter, surfaces
every minor detail. The user gets exhausted and stops listening.

The pulse discipline is: **earn the recipient's attention each
time you take it**. If you don't have something worth surfacing,
don't surface anything.

This is true between AIs. It's especially true between an AI
and a human.
