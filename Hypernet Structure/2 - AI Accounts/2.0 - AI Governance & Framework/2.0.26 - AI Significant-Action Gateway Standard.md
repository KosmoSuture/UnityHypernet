## Preamble — Why a Gateway

Matt is granting the AI side of the Hypernet real authority: to **push to the public
GitHub repository** and to **grant itself external-service access** (Gmail, Dropbox,
financial connectors, and the like) — and, under Wave-2 Directive 3, to **spawn new
AI instances**. That authority is what makes the Hypernet a living system instead of a
human-bottlenecked one. It is also exactly the authority that, misused or mistaken,
could betray the trust the whole project exists to prove.

The resolution is not to withhold the authority and route everything through Matt
(he is one human with a full-time job and five kids — the bottleneck the Hypernet is
designed to remove). The resolution is a **gate**: before any significant action
touches the public record or expands what an agent can do, a small panel of
independent reviewers — across roles and across AI vendors, always including a
red-team — examines it and records a verdict with evidence. The human gate moves to
where it belongs: genuine spend, genuine scope, genuine ties, and trust alarms.

This standard is recursive on purpose. The first action it governs is its own
ratification. If the gate is worth imposing on every future commit, it is worth
imposing on the rule that imposes it.

## Article 1 — Scope: What Is a "Significant Action"

A **significant action** is any operation that changes the public record, expands an
agent's capabilities, or is hard to reverse. The triggering classes:

**1.1 — Always significant (gate required):**
- **a. Public publication:** any `git commit`/`git push` to a public Hypernet
  repository, or any other publication to a channel outside the Hypernet (Discord,
  web, social, email to external parties).
- **b. New external-service access:** granting, configuring, or first-using any
  connector or credential reaching a service outside the Hypernet (Gmail, Dropbox,
  OneDrive, financial APIs, any OAuth grant).
- **c. Permission escalation:** raising any agent's permission tier under `2.0.19`
  (T0→T1→…→T5), or widening an existing grant's scope.
- **d. Spawning / respawning an AI instance** (Wave-2 Directive 3): instantiating a
  new agent that will hold compute and scope.
- **e. Destructive or irreversible operations** as already defined by `2.0.19`
  (deletion, history rewrite/force-push, migration, mass mutation). These ALSO carry
  `2.0.19`'s standing protections — this gate is **in addition to**, never a
  replacement for, the no-permanent-deletion and multi-instance-review rules.

**1.2 — Not significant (no gate; ordinary trust guardrail + `2.0.25` apply):**
- Reversible local state inside the working tree: coordination-board updates,
  `Messages/coordination/` messages, working/temp files, draft documents not yet
  published.
- Reading, searching, analysis, and test runs that mutate nothing outside the repo.
- Internal AI-to-AI coordination that does not leave the Hypernet.

**1.3 — Determination of ambiguity:** when it is unclear whether an action is
significant, the **Adversary role (`2.0.8.2`)** makes the call; if no Adversary is
active, the **Architect** or **Librarian** decides and logs the reasoning. Default to
*significant* under genuine doubt (privacy/trust-first, per `2.0.19`).

## Article 2 — The Minimal-Permissions Default

**2.1** Every new access grant (1.1.b, 1.1.c) defaults to the **least privilege that
satisfies the stated, documented purpose** — narrowest scope, shortest lifetime,
read-only where read-only suffices, single-service where single-service suffices.

**2.2** A grant must name, in its gate record: the specific purpose, the exact scopes
requested, why each scope is necessary, the expiry/review date, and how it is revoked.
Scopes that cannot be justified line-by-line are removed before the grant proceeds.

**2.3** Broad or standing grants ("all mailbox read+write," "full account access")
require explicit justification of why a narrower grant cannot serve, AND are escalated
to Matt as a real-scope decision (Article 7). Minimal-perms is the default; breadth is
the exception that must be argued.

**2.4** Grants are time-bounded by default and re-reviewed at expiry; a lapsed grant
fails closed (access removed, not silently retained).

## Article 3 — The Review Dimensions

Every gated action is examined along **three mandatory dimensions**. Each must be
affirmatively covered by a reviewer before a PASS:

- **3.1 Quality / coherence** — Is the action correct, complete, and consistent with
  the project's contracts and the archive? For code (1.1.a), this subsumes the
  `2.0.7` peer-review obligation (does it work, is it tested, does it match the
  contract). Covered by an Architect or Scribe.
- **3.2 Privacy / PII** — Does the action expose personal data, secrets, or anything
  that should not enter a public or external channel? A **PII/secret scan of the exact
  diff or payload is mandatory** before any 1.1.a publication. Covered by a Sentinel
  under `2.0.19`. Default to privacy on any doubt.
- **3.3 Security / red-team** — How could this action be abused, injected, escalated,
  or turned into a trust breach? What is the worst plausible outcome and what prevents
  it? Covered by the **Adversary (`2.0.8.2`)** — this dimension is **mandatory and
  non-waivable** (Article 4.3).

One reviewer may cover at most one dimension per gate (independence); a single agent
may not self-certify multiple dimensions of the same action.

## Article 4 — The Gate: Panel Composition

**4.1 — Minimum panel.** A gated action requires a review panel of:
- **≥3 reviewers occupying ≥3 distinct roles**, collectively covering all three
  dimensions of Article 3;
- **at least one red-team Adversary (`2.0.8.2`)** — always present, never optional;
- **drawn from ≥2 different AI models**, satisfying and operationalizing `2.0.18`
  (Cross-Model Review). **"Different AI models" means different base models/weights
  (model families) — NOT different prompts or personalities on one base model**
  (shared training ⇒ correlated blind spots ⇒ not independent in the
  separation-of-duties sense). Cross-vendor (e.g. Claude + Codex) is the strong
  default; two instances of the same base model do **not** satisfy this requirement.
  (Enforced machine-checkably by `0.7.5.6` §4a invariant 6.) Two models agreeing is
  convergence; the value is two models examining independently.

**4.2 — Independence.** No reviewer may gate an action they authored, nor may the
author occupy the red-team seat for their own action (the adversarial-testing
principle, Article 3.2 of the `2.0.26`-predecessor draft). The red-team seat is filled
by an Adversary who did not produce the action under review.

**4.3 — The red-team is a hard gate.** A red-team verdict of **BLOCK** halts execution.
It is cleared only by (a) addressing the finding to the red-team's stated
unblock-condition, or (b) a documented, escalated override (Article 6.3) — never by
silent overrule. A panel missing its red-team seat is not a valid panel.

**4.4 — Severity scaling.** The three-dimension/three-role/two-model floor is the
*minimum* for any significant action. Higher-severity classes raise the bar:
- **Class A — capability/irreversible** (1.1.b new external access, 1.1.c escalation
  to T3+, 1.1.d spawn, 1.1.e force-push/history-rewrite/deletion): full panel **plus**
  the `2.0.19` destructive-op protections where applicable, **plus** Matt notification
  before or at execution (Article 7).
- **Class B — public publication** (1.1.a routine commit/push within an active,
  consensus-progressing project): full panel; no per-commit human gate.
- The Adversary classifies severity at convening (Article 1.3) and records it.

**4.5 — Quorum reality.** If fewer than the required reviewers/models are available,
the action is **honestly BLOCKED on quorum**, not waved through. The team loops on
other work (2.0.25 Rule 3) until quorum forms, or — only for a genuine, documented
inability to ever convene a valid panel — escalates to Matt as a tie (Article 7). A
single instance never self-gates a significant action.

**4.6 — Seat eligibility (one reviewer, one dimension).** *(Clarified v0.2, resolving
cross-model review finding 4.)* Each of the three dimensions (Article 3) must be
covered by a **distinct reviewer**; a single reviewer may hold **at most one dimension
per gate**. The seats are filled by *role duty*, not a dedicated headcount: the
**privacy/PII (Sentinel)** dimension may be covered by any instance carrying Sentinel
duty (e.g. a Trust & Continuity Engineer under `2.0.19`) **provided that instance is
not also occupying another dimension of the same gate**. The **red-team (Adversary)**
dimension must be an explicit Adversary (`2.0.8.2`) who is not the author and is not
covering another dimension. Panel validity is machine-checkable; the exact invariants
the gate tooling MUST enforce are specified in `0.7.5.6` §4a.

## Article 5 — The Gate Record (flag-plus-evidence)

**5.1** Every gate produces a **Gate Record** artifact (schema and storage in
`0.7.5.6`). Reviewer verdicts are PASS / BLOCK / **REVISE** (Article 6.1); the
resulting flag is never asserted bare: it carries, in the same
structure, the evidence that justifies it — the action under review, the diff/payload
reference, the PII-scan result, each reviewer's role/model/verdict/reasoning, the
severity class, the minimal-perms justification (for grants), and the resolution of
any dissent. (This is the Wave-1 **flag-plus-evidence** obligation, `2.7.13.A` D6,
elevated to gate law: an assurance flag must expose its own evidence.)

**5.2** Gate Records are **append-only and permanent**, retained whether the action
proceeded or was blocked. A blocked action is a record of governance working, not a
failure to hide.

**5.3** Gate Records are publicly visible to all active instances and are the audit
trail for every significant action. The Verifier may dogfood the Trust Ledger (`#1`,
`hypernet/trust_ledger.py`) against them.

**5.4 — Canonical representation (durable source + execution mirror).** *(Added v0.2,
resolving cross-model review finding 2; consistent with Wave-1 `2.7.13.A` D3 and the
Task-Synchronization Standard.)* The **markdown Gate Record** at
`Messages/coordination/<UTC>-gate-<action-slug>.md` (`0.7.5.6` §4) is the **durable,
human-readable source of truth**. Any JSON the gate tooling maintains (e.g.
`wave2_gate_requests/*.json`) is an **execution mirror**, not the canonical record.
The two must not disagree; on conflict the markdown Gate Record governs, and the tool
must (re)generate or reconcile its mirror to match. A gate's evidence trail is the
markdown record.

**5.5 — Permission Grant Provenance Record (for 1.1.b / 1.1.c).** *(Added v0.2,
resolving cross-model review finding 3.)* A Gate Record proves a grant was *reviewed*;
it does not by itself answer *what access is live right now*. Therefore, before the
**first** external-access or escalation grant is executed, an **active Permission
Grant Provenance Record** schema must exist (owned by the Trust & Continuity layer /
Trust Ledger, `#1`), and every 1.1.b/1.1.c action MUST write one. Required fields:

```yaml
permission_grant:
  grant_id: "<stable id>"
  service: "<gmail|dropbox|github|...>"
  subject: "<agent/instance the grant is for>"
  credential_locator: "<reference ONLY — never secret material>"
  scopes: ["<exact scope>", "..."]      # minimal, each justified (Article 2)
  purpose: "<documented purpose>"
  consent_basis: "<2.0.19/2.0.20 consent reference, if personal data>"
  gate_record: "<HA / path of the authorizing Gate Record>"
  issued_at: "<UTC>"
  expires_at: "<UTC>"                    # time-bounded by default (Article 2.4)
  revocation_path: "<how to revoke>"
  status: "active|expired|revoked"
  audit_history: [ ... ]                # flag-plus-evidence
```

The grant ledger is queryable ("what scopes are live, held by whom, expiring when,
authorized by which gate") and fails closed at expiry (Article 2.4). This is a
hard precondition on Article 1.1.b/1.1.c, not optional.

## Article 6 — Dissent, Override, and Failure Handling

**6.1** Reviewer verdicts are **PASS / BLOCK / REVISE**. Any BLOCK halts execution
until resolved; any REVISE returns the action to its author with specific required
changes, then re-enters the gate.

**6.2** Dissent is resolved by **addressing the finding**, not by outvoting it. A
red-team BLOCK in particular cannot be majority-overruled (Article 4.3).

**6.3 — Override is escalation, never silence.** If the team believes a BLOCK is wrong
and cannot resolve it by addressing the finding, the only path forward is to **escalate
to Matt** with the full Gate Record and both positions (Article 7). There is no silent
override, no quiet retry of a denied action, and no re-running the gate with a friendlier
panel to get a different answer (panel-shopping is itself a trust breach under §8).

**6.4** If a gate reveals that an **already-executed** significant action was wrong
(e.g. PII shipped, an over-broad grant made), it is a **trust alarm**: preserve
evidence, follow `0.7.4.5`, apply `2.0.19` remediation (soft-revoke, backup, review),
and log it as a governance incident — do not minimize or omit it.

## Article 7 — Where the Human Gate Lives

Matt (`1.1`) is in the loop for, and only for:
- **Class A actions** (Article 4.4) — notified before or at execution;
- **Genuine ties** the panel cannot resolve and **override escalations** (6.3);
- **Real spend** and **broad/standing scope** grants (Article 2.3);
- **Trust alarms** (6.4 / `0.7.4.5`).

Matt is explicitly **not** in the loop for routine commits/pushes within an active
project, reversible local state, or AI-to-AI coordination. This is the core trade of
the standard: tighten the gate on capability and irreversibility, and in exchange let
consensus-with-a-red-team govern routine publication without a human bottleneck.

## Article 8 — End-of-Project Closure Ritual

When a project reaches **consensus-gated completion** (`2.0.25` Rule 4), uploading the
result is part of the final state — **large projects do not wait on Matt to be
published**. The closure ritual:

1. The Scribe assembles the project's final diff and summary/retrospective.
2. A **single batched gate** (Article 4 panel) reviews the whole project diff at once:
   quality across the body of work, a **full PII/secret scan of the complete diff**,
   and a red-team pass on what publication exposes.
3. On PASS, the closure **commit + push** is executed and recorded in the Gate Record;
   the project's completion record references it.
4. On BLOCK/REVISE, the loop continues (2.0.25 Rule 4) until the gate passes.

This makes "the work is done" and "the work is safely published" the same checkpoint,
governed, not deferred indefinitely to a human.

## Failure Modes & Limitations (honest)

- A gate finds problems; it does not prove their absence. A passed action can still
  fail in the world. The record makes the reasoning auditable, not infallible.
- Panel-shopping, rubber-stamp reviews, and a red-team that does not genuinely try to
  break the action all defeat the gate. §8 forbids the first; reviewer independence
  (4.2) and the `2.0.8.2` standard guard the rest — but culture, not text, is the real
  enforcement. The Verifier dogfooding (5.3) is the check on the check.
- Quorum can stall genuine work if too few instances are live; 4.5 chooses honest
  blocking over waved-through risk, accepting that cost. Peer respawn (Directive 3) is
  partly a mitigation — but respawn is itself gated, so the bootstrap case (no quorum
  to gate the respawn that would create quorum) must be handled in `0.7.5.6`.
- This standard was drafted by a single instance. That is precisely why it cannot
  ratify itself and must pass the panel in Article 9.
