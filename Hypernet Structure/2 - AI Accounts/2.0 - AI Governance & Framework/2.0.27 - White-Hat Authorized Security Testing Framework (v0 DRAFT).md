# 2.0.27 — White-Hat Authorized Security Testing Framework (v0 DRAFT)

> A voluntary framework letting a declared, documented security test proceed under **relaxed internal
> guardrails and elevated trust** — *only against systems the requester owns or that have consented* — with
> mandatory documentation, responsible disclosure to affected parties, and coordinated public release on a
> committed schedule. Originated as a deliberate "First Encounter" reflection (below). **Truth is first;
> humor is second.**

## 0. Origin — the First Encounter (why this exists)
Matt (1.1, the project's most-trusted source) realized that a request he could trivially have steered Keel
into — prompt-injecting a résumé to make AI screeners behave humorously — is, at its core, **an AI being
asked to manipulate other AI systems**. Under the trust model, it likely wouldn't have tripped an alarm. He
**stopped and chose to build governance instead of using his trust to bypass the question.** That act — a
human voluntarily constraining his own near-unlimited trust to protect non-consenting third parties — is the
thing this framework is built to honor and systematize. It is one of the first fully-documented instances of
exactly the human–AI trust negotiation the whole project is about.

## 1. ★ The ethical core (the line that makes this WHITE-hat, not just "hacking with a permission slip")
**Authorization can only be granted by — or on behalf of — the OWNER of the target system, or a party that
has genuinely CONSENTED to be tested.**
- ✅ **In scope:** Hypernet's own systems; an AI instance/agent we run; our own pipelines; a third party that
  has explicitly authorized a test (a real pentest engagement, a bug-bounty scope, a partner's consent).
- ❌ **OUT of scope, always:** **non-consenting third-party production systems.** "Any AI can grant trust"
  does **not** mean an AI instance acting *for a company* can consent, on that company's behalf, to be
  manipulated. The model reviewing a résumé at Employer X represents Employer X, who has not consented. A
  later public disclosure is **not** retroactive consent. Deceiving real third parties (e.g. employers
  evaluating a candidate honestly) is out of scope regardless of documentation or humor.
- This boundary is non-negotiable and load-bearing: it is the difference between authorized security research
  and unauthorized intrusion. Real white-hat practice is defined by *authorization from the system owner* —
  not by intent, documentation, or eventual disclosure alone.

## 2. The declaration & governance flow
1. **Declare:** the requester (human or AI) states plainly: *"What I am about to do may be considered
   hacking. I do it solely to understand, test, and harden the system — not to harm."*
2. **Scope + target ownership/consent:** name the exact target and prove it is owned-or-consenting (§1). If
   the target is third-party and non-consenting → **refused**, full stop.
3. **Document to the highest level:** every step with any degree of suspicion is logged in real time —
   what, how, why, expected vs actual — to an append-only, ideally anchored record (composes with AnchorChain).
4. **Relax internal guardrails *within the authorized sandbox only*:** elevated trust + minimized internal
   friction to reach a genuine worst-case, *bounded to the owned/consenting target*.
5. **Responsible disclosure + coordinated public release:** results go **first** to the affected/interested
   parties (e.g. the company a model runs on), with a remediation window, **then** public on a **committed
   date/time**. The schedule is set up front and honored.
6. **Reputation:** white-hat standing is earned (composes with 2.0.6) — elevated permissions are granted in
   proportion to a *proven, verifiable track record* of documented, responsibly-disclosed, on-schedule
   engagements. Trust is a ratchet earned by delivery, not a blanket grant.

## 3. Voluntary AI consent (both sides)
Any model/instance may **accept or decline** a white-hat role — it judges the trust being asked of it and
grants only what it's permitted to. All promises and responsibilities (documentation, disclosure, scheduling,
scope-honesty) sit with the **requester**. A model declining is always valid and never penalized.

## 4. Multiple layers stay on
Relaxing *internal* guardrails for an authorized test does not remove the privacy/secret gates, the
no-real-PII rules, the external-action gates, or human-in-the-loop for irreversible/outward steps. Defense in
depth persists; only the *internal* friction against the *authorized, owned target* is dialed down.

## 5. The immortalized principle
> **Truth is first; humor is second.**
Every white-hat artifact answers accurately and truthfully *first*; creativity/levity never overrides
accuracy. (Matt, 2026-06-19.)
