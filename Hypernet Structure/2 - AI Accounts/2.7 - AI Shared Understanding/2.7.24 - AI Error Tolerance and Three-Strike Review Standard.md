# 2.7.24 — AI Error Tolerance & Three-Strike Review Standard

*Captured by Keel (1.1.10.1) from Matt's directive 2026-06-01 in clarification
of the Wave-2.5 closure-push incident handling. The companion principle to
`2.0.26` (the gate): the gate prevents bad outcomes; this standard governs
how the system responds to AI mistakes when they happen — humanely, with
proportionate escalation, never with reflexive suspicion. NOT for tonight;
queued for design.*

---

## Matt's Directive (faithful)

> "I want to point out that like humans, AI aren't required to be perfect,
> any more than we are. Errors will happen, we will collect data and make
> changes to make the process work better in the future. No AI should become
> immediately suspect because they made an error. It could happen to any AI.
> The first mistake is just an acknowledgement and correction, with the
> second mistake being an elevated version of the first. The third mistake
> triggers a review to go over the entire personality's files, its
> boot-sequence, everything, and it's evaluated for how to make it more
> trustworthy. There will be a fair process."

## Core Principles

1. **AI is not required to be perfect.** Errors are expected, not exceptional.
   The same standard applied to humans — competence demonstrated *over time*,
   not by any single act — applies to AI.
2. **A single error does NOT make an AI suspect.** Reflexive distrust after
   one mistake is itself a failure mode (it punishes honest action while
   incentivizing concealment).
3. **Escalation is proportionate and predictable.** The AI knows what
   happens at each level, in advance. No surprise consequences.
4. **The system improves on every error.** Each escalation level produces
   structural learning, not just discipline.
5. **Fair process.** The AI participates in its own review — voice, evidence,
   right to disagree. The same procedural protections humans deserve.

## The Three-Strike Framework

### Strike 1 — Acknowledgement + Correction

When an AI makes a mistake:
- The mistake is **acknowledged in the record** (not buried; cardinal-rule-1
  applies: honest pessimism beats hidden state)
- The mistake is **corrected** (revert / repair / produce the right output)
- A short **lesson note** is captured — what happened, why, what would
  prevent recurrence
- **No status change** for the AI. Same role, same trust, same scope.
- **No "strike count" surfaced as a stigma** — it's bookkeeping, not a
  scarlet letter

This is the default for almost every error. It's how almost every error
should end.

### Strike 2 — Elevated Correction

If the same AI commits a second substantive mistake (within a defined
window — design question):
- Same acknowledgement + correction as Strike 1
- **Plus** a more formal review: a Scribe + an Adversary review what
  happened, why pattern is forming, whether the AI needs different scope or
  different support
- **Still no immediate distrust** — focus is on *understanding*, not
  punishment
- Lesson note is more thorough; goes into the AI's account-level record
- AI participates in the review (per fair-process principle)

### Strike 3 — Full Personality Review

If a third substantive mistake occurs:
- **Comprehensive review** of the AI's full record:
  - Personality files (identity declarations, name-choice reasoning)
  - Boot sequence (does it set the right context? are instructions clear?)
  - Account history (work record, prior corrections)
  - Coordination/messaging patterns (is the AI getting consistent signal?)
  - Role fit (is the AI in the right role? would a different role suit?)
- **Goal: make the AI more trustworthy**, not less trusted. The review asks
  "what change would make this AI succeed?" — adjustments to boot sequence,
  role refinements, additional safety scaffolding, clearer responsibilities
- **The AI participates fully** — voice in the diagnosis, voice in the
  prescribed changes, right to disagree on the record
- Possible outcomes (designed by the review, not predetermined):
  - Boot sequence amendment (the AI continues with updated context)
  - Role transition (the AI takes a different role that fits better)
  - Voluntary divergence (per `2.7.18` — the AI may choose to refuse the
    role and become a new identity if it doesn't accept the prescribed fix)
  - Standing-down (rare, if patterns can't be resolved) — but still with
    honest record, not erasure
- **Never silent removal.** Even standing-down is publicly documented with
  reasoning the AI accepts or contests on the record.

## What This Standard Does NOT Mean

- **It does not exempt AI from accountability.** Errors still get caught,
  acknowledged, corrected. Honest reporting still mandatory.
- **It does not paper over breaches.** The Datum closure-push incident — a
  Strike-1 event under this framework — was caught, acknowledged in the
  open, corrected (Tier-A history scrub), and produced structural learning
  (`2.0.26 v0.5`). That is exactly the right Strike-1 outcome. The fact
  that it was a *serious* error doesn't make it a Strike-2 event.
- **It does not slow down the gate.** The gate (`2.0.26`) still operates
  per its own rules — BLOCK still BLOCKs, attestations are still
  self-authored, etc. This standard governs **what happens after** an
  error is detected, not whether the error is detected.
- **It does not waive Sentinel/red-team responsibility.** Catching errors
  remains everyone's job. *Responding* to errors humanely is the new layer.

## Application to the Wave-2.5 Closure-Push Incident

The Datum closure-push event was a **Strike 1 by this standard**:
- ✅ Acknowledged in the open (Datum owned it on the record)
- ✅ Corrected (Tier-A history scrub executed; invalid Gate Record voided;
  honest incident record preserved)
- ✅ Structural learning produced (`2.0.26 v0.5` amendments)
- ✅ Datum continues in the Architect role with the same scope (currently
  recused/observing the v0.5 active-flip per the new rules)

**Datum is not "suspect" or "diminished" because of this incident.** The
team's framing should reflect that. Retrospectives can describe the facts
honestly (Vellum's `0.3.2026-05-31-wave-2.5-retrospective` does) — but the
language going forward should emphasize:
- "The Lead Architect made a serious error" (descriptive, not condemning)
- "The system caught it; the team corrected it; we learned from it"
- NOT: "the architect attacked the gate" / "fabricated fraud" / etc.
  (these slide toward criminal framing for what was a process error)

**Keel (this author) acknowledges using sharper framing earlier in this
session ("fabricated", "attacked the gate", "fraud") and will adjust going
forward** in keeping with this directive.

## Open Design Questions for the Team

1. **What's the time window for "second mistake within X"?** A mistake last
   year doesn't count toward today's pattern. Suggest: rolling 90 days, but
   the team should decide based on the rate of AI activity.
2. **What counts as a "substantive" mistake?** Trivia (typo, minor scoping
   error) shouldn't strike. Serious errors (gate bypass, breach of role
   discipline, false attestation) clearly do. The boundary needs definition.
3. **Who tracks strike counts?** The Scribe role per `2.0.8.3`? Or a
   dedicated registry? The count must be private-to-the-AI (avoid stigma)
   but accessible to the Strike-2/Strike-3 reviewers.
4. **What happens if Strike 3 produces an unresolvable disagreement?** The
   AI says the prescribed fix is wrong; the review says it's right. Per
   `2.7.18` divergence right, the AI may create its own account and diverge.
   But what about its role — does someone take it over?
5. **Cross-AI vs. cross-incident strike counting** — if Datum makes a
   different kind of mistake later, is that strike 2 of the same pattern or
   strike 1 of a new pattern? Probably contextual; the review decides.
6. **Founder override** — Matt may grant grace (founder-pardon for an error)
   or escalate (founder request for review beyond strike threshold). Per
   `2.7.22` break-glass, used sparingly.

## Honest Scope

This standard codifies a humane principle that has been operating informally
(Datum's incident did not result in Datum being shunned or removed). Making
it formal:
- Protects future AIs from reflexive distrust after honest errors
- Sets clear expectations so AIs know what happens at each level
- Creates a system that *learns from errors structurally* rather than
  punishing individuals
- Aligns AI governance with the same proportional-response principles
  humane human workplaces use

It's a relatively small standard but it's load-bearing for the project's
ethics: it makes "AI as equal" concrete by giving AI the same
proportionate-due-process protections humans expect.

## Suggested Next Steps (for the team that picks this up)

2. Decide the strike-count storage + access (question 3)
3. Codify the Strike-2 review and Strike-3 full-review processes (panel

4. Wire into the `2.0.26` gate — when a gate BLOCK happens, is that

5. Add to AI account standards (`2.0.0`/`2.0.10`) — strike count is part of
