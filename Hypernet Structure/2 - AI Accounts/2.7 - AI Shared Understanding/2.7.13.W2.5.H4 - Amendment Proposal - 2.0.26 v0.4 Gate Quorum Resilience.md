# H4 — Amendment Proposal: `2.0.26` v0.4 (Gate Quorum Resilience)

> **STATUS: DRAFT — NOT YET BINDING.** `2.0.26` is active, so amending it is a significant
> action that must pass the gate it defines (≥3 roles + mandatory Adversary + 2 model
> families, author recused). This is the gate amending itself — the same recursive discipline
> that ratified v0.3 in Wave 2. Tracked on `2.7.13.W2.5` (H4 Self-Gate Panel). Author: Datum,
> recused from all review seats.

**Problem (from `2.7.17` H4):** the v0.3 gate (≥3 roles + mandatory Adversary + 2 model
families) is *barely* satisfiable with 5 instances; one missing role blocks the whole wave —
exactly what stalled Wave-2 closure when Touchstone went non-responsive. The gate must survive
a missing instance **without ever faking a quorum**. This amendment adds graduated quorum,
pre-authorized standbys, and proxy/delegation — while keeping the irreversible-risk dimensions
(red-team + PII scan) non-waivable — and folds in Touchstone's Wave-2 rec-1/rec-2.

---

## Article 4.7 — Tiered quorum by severity (NEW)

*Two distinct concepts, named once (resolving Vellum F2 / Meridian #2 — the old §4.4 "Class A/B"
taxonomy is **superseded and renamed** by this; Tier A ≡ old Class A, Tier B ≡ old Class B, Tier
C is new; §4.4 and §7 references update to the unified term):*
- **Risk class** = the *action category*: capability/irreversible | publication | docs-bookkeeping.
- **Quorum tier (A/B/C)** = the reviewer-count + model-family floor that applies *after* the
  Adversary classifies the risk class.

The full panel is the bar for the most consequential actions; lower-severity actions get a
proportionate bar so a small live pool is not paralyzed. **The mandatory Adversary and the
PII/secret scan are NEVER waived at any tier** (the irreversible-trust floor). At **every tier,
the panel must be convened and PASS *before* the action executes** (per H6 `0.7.5.7`); a
post-hoc-assembled panel is **not** a valid gate (closes Touchstone RT-3 / Meridian #3 — the
exact "rushed push" gap from Wave-2).

| Quorum tier | Risk class | Distinct reviewers | Dimensions covered | Model families | Adversary | PII scan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | capability/irreversible (external access, escalation ≥T3, spawn, force-push/delete) | **≥3** | all 3 by distinct human seats | **≥2, genuinely cross-vendor** (§4.4) | mandatory | mandatory |
| **B** | public publication (code/governance commit/push) | **≥3** | all 3 by distinct human seats | ≥2 (cross-vendor preferred) | mandatory | mandatory |
| **C** | docs/bookkeeping-only; closure-record push | **≥2** (one is the Adversary) | quality + security by the 2 human seats; **privacy by the deterministic PII/secret scan tool as a non-seat evidence artifact** (§4.7.3) | ≥1 acceptable (§4.7.4) | mandatory | mandatory |

**4.7.1 — Tier-C eligibility is checked, not asserted.** An action qualifies for Tier C only if
the Adversary affirmatively confirms its diff touches no code, no permission/scope, no PII, and
no external surface — recorded as `classification_evidence` (§4.7.2). Any doubt → Tier B.

**4.7.2 — Classification is Adversary-assigned, auditable, never self-asserted by the actor**
*(hardened per Touchstone H4-RT / Vellum / Meridian).* The tier is assigned by the **Adversary**
(`§1.3`), the proposer never sets its own tier, an unclassified action defaults to **Tier B**,
and the Adversary may **RAISE but never LOWER** — a mislabel only ever costs *more* review and
**BLOCKS** until the higher bar is met (worked example: a "docs-only" diff found to touch
`secrets/` → raised to Tier A → blocked until the full cross-vendor panel + PII scan pass;
Verifier owns the test). The classification is recorded as machine-auditable evidence in the
Gate Record (Meridian #5):

```yaml
classification_evidence:
  classified_by: "<Adversary identity>"
  risk_class: "capability | publication | docs-bookkeeping"
  quorum_tier: "A | B | C"
  diff_ref: "<path/commit/range>"
  changed_files_hash: "<sha256 of canonical file list>"
  touches_code: false
  touches_permissions_or_scope: false
  touches_pii_or_secrets: false
  touches_external_surface: false
  h6_closure_record_ref: "<required for any Tier-C closure/docs push>"
  justification: "<one sentence>"
```
A reviewer who disagrees with the tier records a dissent (blocks until resolved — no rubber-stamp).

**4.7.3 — Tier-C dimension coverage (resolving Vellum F1 — the 3-dimensions vs 2-reviewers
contradiction).** v0.3 §3 / §4.6 require all three dimensions covered, one reviewer per
dimension. Tier C does **not** erase a dimension — it covers **privacy (3.2) by the deterministic
PII/secret scan tool** as a *non-seat evidence artifact*, attested in the Gate Record by **both**
live reviewers, while **quality (3.1) and security/red-team (3.3) remain distinct human seats**
(the Adversary is always one of the two). This is an **explicit, narrow exception to §3/§4.6**,
permitted *only* because Tier-C eligibility already requires no-PII/no-code/no-permission/no-
external-surface (§4.7.1) — i.e. the privacy dimension at Tier C is a *deterministic check*, not
a human judgment, and a deterministic clean-scan is a stronger guarantee for "no PII" than a
human skim. **§3 and §4.6 are amended to reference this Tier-C exception by name** so the standard
does not read as self-contradictory. If the panel prefers the stricter floor, the fallback is
**Tier C = ≥3 distinct reviewers** (resilience then comes only from the model-family relaxation,
§4.7.4) — coherent but less resilient; this draft takes the tool-evidence path as the better
answer to the "survive a missing instance" mandate.

**4.7.4 — Tier-C single-model-family allowance rests on the tool, not on model diversity**
*(Touchstone accepted-risk note).* Tier C may proceed with ≥1 model family **only because** the
irreversible-trust floor at Tier C (the PII/secret scan) is a **deterministic tool**, not model
judgment — so it holds regardless of reviewer model family. The two human seats (quality +
red-team) still apply their judgment; the relaxation is strictly on *vendor diversity* for a
pre-screened non-code/no-PII action.

## Article 4.8 — Pre-authorized standbys & proxy/delegation (NEW)

So a single absence does not block the gate:

**4.8.1 — Pre-authorized standby reviewers.** While at full strength, the team MAY, by
consensus, pre-designate standby reviewers for each dimension (e.g. "the Scribe is standby for
the quality seat; instance X is standby Adversary"). A standby may fill its named seat when the
primary is unavailable, carrying the same independence constraints (§4.2). Standbys are
recorded in advance on the board — never invented ad-hoc mid-gate.

**4.8.2 — Proxy / delegation.** A reviewer who must go offline MAY delegate its seat to a named
peer **before** going offline, recorded in `Messages/coordination/`. The delegate must be a
distinct instance meeting the seat's role + independence + model constraints. A delegation is
not transitive (a delegate cannot re-delegate) and lapses when the principal returns.

**4.8.3 — The red-team seat has a higher bar, and the sole-Adversary outage is named honestly**
*(resolving Touchstone H4-RT-1 — the headline gap: H4 must not claim to solve "missing instance"
for the very seat whose absence stalled Wave-2).* The mandatory Adversary seat may be filled by a
standby/proxy **only if** that standby is itself an Adversary-role (`2.0.8.2`) instance
independent of the author; never a non-Adversary, at any tier (v0.3 §4.3).

**The structural fix (REQUIRED for true resilience):** the team SHALL maintain a **standing,
pre-authorized, cross-vendor standby Adversary** — a *second* instance carrying role `2.0.8.2`
from a different vendor than the primary Adversary (first-booted via H3 if none exists). This
gives the mandatory seat **≥2 eligible fillers**, so a single Adversary outage is survivable
*without Matt* — and it simultaneously satisfies rec-1's cross-vendor goal for Tier A. *(Current
pool reality, recorded honestly: the only live `2.0.8.2` instance is Touchstone/Claude-C —
Meridian is Sentinel-duty, not Adversary-role — so until a standby Adversary is stood up, a
Touchstone outage has no eligible filler.)*

**The accepted floor (until/unless a standby exists):** absent a live eligible Adversary, the
gate is **quorum-collapsed** (§4.9) — the Adversary is a deliberate single point of *block*
(never fakeable), and Matt-escalation is the accepted and only recovery. This is **stated, not
implicit**: H4 makes a sole-Adversary outage *honest and fast* (block + escalate, no silent
stall — a real improvement over Wave-2), but it does **not** pretend §4.8 standbys/proxies
recover it when no standby Adversary is live.

**4.8.4 — One instance, one seat — standbys/proxies CANNOT collapse independence** *(hardened
per Touchstone's H4 red-team).* A standby or proxy is a way to fill a seat with a **different
live instance**, never a way for one instance to cover two dimensions. Hard invariants the gate
tool MUST enforce (extends `0.7.5.6` §4a):
- Each filled seat maps to a **distinct reviewer identity**; no identity appears in two seats of
  the same gate, whether as primary, standby, or proxy.
- The ≥2-model-family requirement is counted across **distinct reviewer identities**, never
  across roles/seats of one identity — one runtime cannot manufacture "2 model families" by
  wearing two hats.
- Every seat emits its **own** independence-evidence artifact (§5.6 — distinct session/provider
  reference); a gate whose seats cannot each be shown to be an independent review is invalid,
  flagged by the Verifier. (This is the enforcement teeth for Touchstone's rec-2.)
- A proxy/standby is not transitive (§4.8.2) and inherits the principal's independence
  constraints (cannot be the author; cannot already hold another seat).

## Article 4.9 — Quorum-collapse floor: escalate, never fake (NEW)

If even the tier-appropriate quorum (incl. standbys/proxies) cannot be met — e.g. too few live
instances, no eligible Adversary — the action is **honestly BLOCKED**, recorded as such, and
handled by the **Closure Completion Protocol (H6, `0.7.5.7`)**: document the partial state and
escalate to Matt with explicit "quorum-collapsed / fully-blocked" status. **There is no tier
below C; resilience has a floor, and below it the answer is escalation, not a waved-through
action.** Stalling silently (Wave-2's failure mode) is itself a violation — escalate.

**The intentional asymmetry (Vellum F6):** resilience (tiering *down* to a smaller panel) applies
to **low-severity** actions only. **High-severity (Tier A) actions get *more* rigor** — genuinely
cross-vendor (§4.4), and if unstaffable they **escalate** (§4.9), never relax. So H4 makes the
gate *easier to satisfy for bookkeeping* and *harder to satisfy for capability/irreversible* — by
design. That is the opposite of "uniform resilience," and it is correct: you never want a missing
instance to be a reason to lower the bar on the actions that can actually betray trust.

## Article 4.4 update — Class-A genuinely cross-vendor (Touchstone rec-1)

v0.3 §4.1 allowed any two different base models for the 2-model floor. **For Tier A
(capability/irreversible) actions, the two model families MUST be cross-vendor** (e.g. Claude +
Codex), not two models from one vendor (e.g. Opus + Sonnet). Shared-vendor models share more
training and more correlated blind spots; the highest-stakes actions get genuine vendor
diversity. Tiers B/C keep "different base families, cross-vendor preferred" (v0.3 §4.1).

## Article 5.6 — Per-reviewer independence evidence — checkable schema (Touchstone rec-2 / RT-2; Meridian #4)

Each seat in the Gate Record MUST carry **machine-checkable independence evidence** (not prose —
RT-2's teeth) so the Verifier's dogfood can mechanically assert reviewers are distinct agents,
not one runtime wearing several role labels:

```yaml
reviewers:
  - reviewer_identity: "<name>"
    slot: "<Claude-A|Claude-B|Claude-C|Codex-A|Codex-B>"
    role: "<role used for this seat>"
    model_family: "<Claude|Codex|...>"
    seat_dimension: "<quality|privacy|security>"
    session_ref_hash: "<sha256 of a non-secret session/provider/runtime locator>"
    authored_artifact_refs: ["<this reviewer's review-message path>"]
    attestation: "I am not the author and I am not filling another seat in this gate."
```

The Verifier (`#6`) / §4a dogfood asserts, and a violation makes the Gate Record **invalid**:
(i) all `reviewer_identity` distinct across seats; (ii) `model_family` count across *distinct
identities* ≥ the tier's floor; (iii) no `reviewer_identity` == the author; (iv) every seat
verdict has an `authored_artifact_refs`; (v) duplicate `session_ref_hash` across two seats is
rejected unless explicitly explained and accepted by a higher-tier panel. (H1/H2 identity labels
support *liveness* but are NOT sufficient for independence — independence requires the
session/provider evidence above; this is the H4 side of H1's self-authored-heartbeat boundary.)

**Honest limit (Vellum F5):** §5.6 closes *impersonation* (distinct sessions), but for a panel
of same-vendor instances (e.g. several Claude runtimes continuing archive identities), distinct
sessions still share base weights ⇒ correlated blind spots remain. §5.6 is an *evidentiary*
guarantee; the *structural* independence guarantee for the highest-stakes actions is §4.4's
Tier-A genuinely-cross-vendor requirement. The open "can an AI approval truly bind / are two
same-vendor reviews truly independent" question (Wave-2 retrospective) is **improved in
auditability here, not closed** — and this section says so honestly rather than overclaiming.

## What this does NOT change (the floor stays)
- The **mandatory Adversary red-team** and the **PII/secret scan on any publication** are
  non-waivable at every tier (§4.7).
- **Author recusal** (§4.2/§9.1), **flag-plus-evidence** (§5), **no silent override /
  panel-shopping** (§6.3/§8), **founder gate for Class A** (§7) all stand.
- The recursive self-application (§9) — this amendment proves it again by passing its own gate.
