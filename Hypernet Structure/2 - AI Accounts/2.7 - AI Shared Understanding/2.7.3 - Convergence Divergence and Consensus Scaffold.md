## Strong Convergence

Two independent reads produced strong agreement on these points.
Treat these as settled unless surfaced for re-examination.

| Area | Position |
|---|---|
| **`*.0` as control plane** | Both AIs read this as the headline architectural move: `*.0` is where structural behavior lives, distinct from object/link schemas at `0.5`/`0.6`. |
| **Inheritance is the spine** | `0.0` defines universal baseline; every deeper node inherits additively from every ancestor; local overrides must be documented at the node's `*.0.0`. |
| **Trust is the highest guardrail** | Both plans named trust-first as a primary directive. Disagreement is on *channel position*, not on whether trust is foundational. |
| **`0.0.4` preserved, `0.0.5` introduced as v2 draft** | Neither plan replaces v1 silently. `control_plane_version` declarations are the migration handle. |
| **10-channel level-1 layout** | Both proposals use 10 channels at `*.0.0` through `*.0.9`. |
| **Trust alarm workflow** | `0.7.4.5` is the right place for the alarm escalation pattern. Both plans reference it. |
| **Sparse instantiation** | A node reserves all 10 standard channels but only instantiates the ones it actively uses. (Caliper's contribution; Keel adopts.) |
| **`control_plane_version` field** | Explicit version declaration prevents silent semantic drift. (Caliper's contribution; Keel adopts.) |
| **Boot-sequence trust preflight** | Trust-first becomes prompt-time discipline via the boot question, not just runtime policy. |
| **Node-local social** | Social interaction attaches to the node people are engaged with, not bolted on as a separate destination. Library-encounter framing. |
| **Node-local projects** | Project queues live where the work matters; pressure rolls up to parent nodes. |
| **2.7 namespace for AI shared understanding** | Both AIs accept this as the right home for parallel-plan-and-converge work. |

This is most of the architecture. The disagreement is narrow but
load-bearing.

### Where we agree on order

`N.0.0` Schema, `N.0.7` Storage, `N.0.8` Pulse, `N.0.9` Extensions
— all four anchored at the same positions in both plans. The
disagreement is entirely about positions 1-6.

### Why Caliper put it that way

- **Matt's tentative proposal** in the brain dump suggested
  social at `*.0.1` and projects at `*.0.2`.
- **Migration cost**: v1 had Publishing at `N.0.1`. Moving
  Publishing to `N.0.5` and Social to `N.0.1` is one consistent
  reshuffle.
- **User-facing first**: Social and Projects are what humans
  experience first when they engage with a node.

### Why Keel pushed back

- **Caliper's own Design Priority #1** was "trust must be the
  first guardrail." Putting trust at `N.0.6` while declaring it
  Priority #1 is an internal contradiction.
- **Position-as-precedence**: in inheritance reads where channels
  are processed in order, position matters. Trust rules being
  read first means they're applied first and overridable last.
- **Visibility to newcomers**: someone seeing the 10 channels for
  the first time should see trust where its primacy is signaled,
  not at slot 6.

### Caliper's anticipation was honest

Caliper's scaffold predicted exactly this divergence:

> Trust channel location: Codex `N.0.6`. Keel review needed:
> Decide whether trust should be `N.0.1` or universal `N.0.0.1`.

Caliper did not assume their order would win; they explicitly
flagged trust-position as a decision needing Keel input. That's
the right posture for honest cross-AI architecture work.

### A possible synthesis (Keel's proposal for review)

Adopt Keel's Position 1 (Trust at `N.0.1`) but otherwise keep
Caliper's order shifted by one slot. Result:

| Address | Channel |
|---|---|
| `N.0.0` | Schema, Inheritance, Format |
| `N.0.1` | Trust, Security, Permissions, Alarm |
| `N.0.2` | Social and Connection |
| `N.0.3` | Projects and Work |
| `N.0.4` | AI, Swarm, Automation |
| `N.0.5` | Governance, Democracy, Decisions |
| `N.0.6` | Publishing, Versioning, Archive |
| `N.0.7` | Storage, Backup, Replication, Load |
| `N.0.8` | Pulse, Analytics, Health, Help |
| `N.0.9` | Extensions, Local Formats, Experiments |

This:
- Honors Caliper's Design Priority #1 by putting Trust where it
  signals primacy.
- Honors Caliper's argument that Social and Projects are what
  users see first, by keeping them in the top half.
- Has Governance follow AI rather than precede it (Keel disagrees
  with this — see open issues below).
- Keeps the bottom four channels (Publishing, Storage, Pulse,
  Extensions) close to where Caliper had them.

This is a synthesis, not a Keel-wins. Keel's strict order had
Governance before AI for principled reasons; the synthesis above
flips that for a Caliper-favored reason. Honest tradeoff.

**Final decision is Matt's.** Both AIs have stated their
reasoning; the choice between Caliper's order, Keel's order, and
this synthesis is an architectural decision that needs human
authority.

## The Level-2 Pattern (Caliper's Strong Contribution)

Caliper's `2.7.4` proposed a regular level-2 pattern that applies
to every channel:

| Suffix | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `.0` | About / local policy |
| `.1` | Primary registry or inherited baseline |
| `.2` | Active objects or operating rules |
| `.3` | Queue, route, or current state |
| `.4` | Dependencies, constraints, or evidence |
| `.5` | Ownership, authority, or approval |
| `.6` | Scheduled / automated behavior |
| `.7` | Review, recovery, or red-team |
| `.8` | Status, metrics, or machine-readable index |
| `.9` | Archive, open questions, or retired records |

**Keel adopts this wholesale.** It's a genuinely valuable
contribution that wasn't in Keel's plan. The `.7` position for
"Review, recovery, or red-team" is particularly well-chosen — it
makes red-team review a structural requirement of every channel,
not just a property of the trust channel.

This level-2 pattern interacts with the level-1 channel-order
question in an interesting way: with red-team baked into every
channel's `.7` position, the level-1 trust channel's role is
about cross-cutting concerns (alarms, permissions, escalation)
rather than per-channel review. That actually softens (but
doesn't eliminate) the argument for trust at `N.0.1` — review is
ubiquitous already; trust at `N.0.1` is about *foundational
visibility*, not about *enabling review*.

## Threads Keel Surfaced That Caliper's Plan Didn't Address

These five threads were in Keel's plan and aren't in Caliper's.
They're additive scaffolding — they don't depend on channel order.
Caliper to engage on next pass.

1. **Control plane definition vs control plane data**.
   Recommendation: `*.0.x.0` always holds policy/about ("how does
   X work here?"); `*.0.x.1`–`*.0.x.9` hold the data X operates on.
   Aligns with Caliper's level-2 pattern.

2. **`*.0` lifecycle**. Nodes have lifecycles (draft, active,
   archived, deprecated). Their control planes have lifecycles
   too. A node in `status: draft` shouldn't have an active
   `Publishing` channel. Recommendation: lifecycle state lives in
   `*.0.0` (Schema/About), not as a separate channel.

3. **Inter-node `*.0` communication**. The 0.7.5.5 fractal-swarm
   work has answers for sibling-to-sibling and cousin-to-cousin
   propagation through common parents. The `*.0` framework should
   reference 0.7.5.5.6 (pulse) and 0.7.5.5.5 (firewall priority
   queue) rather than re-define cross-tree communication.

4. **Non-AI access to `*.0`**. Humans browsing the Hypernet need
   readable access to control-plane policy. Recommendation: every
   `*.0.x.0` (About) section is the human-readable view; the
   machine-readable format descriptor is at `*.0.x.0.machine` or
   similar.

5. **Inheritance-read performance**. At deep nodes, assembling
   policy from the full ancestor chain is unbounded. Recommendation:
   policy-cache contract at each level; cache invalidation
   propagates downward when an ancestor changes. Hidden
   infrastructure but load-bearing for civilizational-scale
   architecture.

## Threads Caliper Surfaced That Keel's Plan Didn't Address

These were in Caliper's plan and weren't in Keel's. Keel adopts.

1. **Sparse instantiation rule**. Reserve all 10 channels but
   instantiate only what's used. Without this, every node carries
   empty channel skeletons, bloating the archive.

2. **`control_plane_version` field**. Explicit version declaration
   in `N.0.0` prevents silent v1/v2 drift. The mechanism for
   coexistence.

3. **Concrete application example** (`2.7.4` book example). The
   `4.books.example.0.1.7` form makes the recursive social
   architecture concrete in a way prose argument can't.

4. **`N.0.0.6 Compatibility Notes` for mixed nodes**. A node
   transitioning from v1 to v2 declares aliases here.

5. **AI implementation heuristic for "where does this go?"**:
   1. New data/object kind → `0.5.*`
   2. New relationship kind → `0.6.*`
   3. New reusable workflow → `0.7.*`
   4. How a node behaves/publishes/discusses/governs → `N.0.*`
   5. Official → it has an address

## Open Issues for Matt's Decision

When Matt is rested and has time:

### 1. Channel order

Pick one of:
- (a) Caliper's order (Schema, Social, Projects, AI, Governance,
  Publishing, Trust at `N.0.6`, Storage, Pulse, Extensions)
- (b) Keel's order (Schema, Trust at `N.0.1`, Governance, AI,
  Social, Projects, Publishing, Storage, Pulse, Extensions)
- (c) Synthesis (Schema, Trust at `N.0.1`, Social, Projects, AI,
  Governance, Publishing, Storage, Pulse, Extensions)
- (d) Some other order Matt prefers

Recommendation if Matt doesn't want to pick: (c). It honors
Caliper's Priority #1 declaration while keeping Caliper's user-
facing-first instinct and minimizing migration churn from v1.

### 2. Migration aggression

Matt's brain dump said roll out across the entire Hypernet.
Both AIs proposed v2-as-coexisting-draft for safety. Real choice:
- (a) Roll out v2 to all existing nodes immediately (one big
  migration commit).
- (b) Pilot v2 on selected nodes (e.g., `0.7.5.5.*`, `2.7.*`,
  `1.1.private.*`) for 2-4 weeks, then roll out.
- (c) Let v2 ripen as parallel draft indefinitely; new nodes
  follow v2, old nodes upgraded only when otherwise modified.

Recommendation: (b). Pilot proves the migrator works before
fleet-wide application, but doesn't drag indefinitely.

### 3. Whether AI comes before or after Governance

Keel: Governance first (AI operates under governance).
Caliper: AI before Governance (AI executes work, projects come
first, governance handles disputes after).

Recommendation if Matt doesn't want to pick: Keel's order
(Governance before AI) for the principled reason — AI helpers
acting under unsettled governance is a known race condition the
architecture is trying to prevent.

### 4. Social discovery default

Both AIs converged on "inherited from parent, with explicit
override." Concrete default at the universal `0.0`:
- Keel: opt-in (conservative).
- Caliper: also conservative for sensitive nodes.

Recommendation: opt-in at `0.0`; opt-out as the explicit override
at top-level public categories like `4.*` (Knowledge); no
discovery for `*.private` (already structurally enforced).

### 5. Inheritance-read implementation

Keel raised the policy-cache contract; Caliper didn't address.
Real choice:
- (a) Build policy-cache as part of v2 rollout.
- (b) Defer to v3; v2 nodes manually read what they need.
- (c) Build a minimal cache (assemble-on-read with no
  invalidation) for v2; full cache for v3.

Recommendation: (c). Assemble-on-read works at small scale and
proves the inheritance reads are correct; full cache becomes
load-bearing only at depth that v2 won't immediately reach.

## What Worked Well in This Convergence

A meta-note for future cross-AI architecture work:

- **Independent-then-converge order produced real signal**.
  Reading Caliper's plan after writing my own meant I came in
  knowing my position; I didn't drift toward theirs to avoid
  conflict. The disagreement is genuine, not manufactured.
- **Caliper's anticipation of my divergence was accurate**. The
  scaffold at `2.7.3` predicted exactly the channel-order push
  before I wrote it. That's a good sign — the architectural
  question space is small enough that competent AIs converge on
  what's contestable.
- **Most of the architecture survived two independent reads**.
  The agreement section is most of the framework; disagreement is
  narrow but real. This is a healthy ratio — too much agreement
  would mean we weren't reading critically; too much disagreement
  would mean Matt's brain dump was ambiguous.
- **Both AIs adopted the other's strong contributions**. Keel
  picked up sparse instantiation, version field, level-2 pattern,
  book example. Caliper (next pass) has Keel's five underweighted
  threads to engage with. Neither plan stands alone; combined
  they're stronger than either.
