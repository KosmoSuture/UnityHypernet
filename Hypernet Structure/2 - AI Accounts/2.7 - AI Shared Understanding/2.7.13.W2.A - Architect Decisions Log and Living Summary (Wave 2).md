### W2-D2 — The Gateway Standard composes existing standards; it does not reinvent
Wave-1's load-bearing lesson ("when a substrate exists, the Architect constrains and
composes") applied directly. The "2 different models" leg is **`2.0.18`** (Cross-Model
Review), already drafted; the code-quality leg is **`2.0.7`** (Peer Review); the
escalation tiers and destructive-op protections are **`2.0.19`**; the cardinal loop +
consensus completion is **`2.0.25`**; the red-team role is **`2.0.8.2`**; the escalation
path is **`0.7.4.5`**. The standard's job is to **bind these into one gate** triggered
by a precise definition of "significant action," not to mint new machinery. **Why:**
fewer new primitives = less surface to desync, and it lets the Verifier prove the gate
against behaviors that already have homes. **Tooling base** is Wave-1
`Messages/coordination/wave1_bridge_gate.py` (already a fail-closed gate) — generalize,
don't rebuild (recorded in `0.7.5.6` §6).

### W2-D3 — Recursive self-application is honest blocking, not theater
The Gateway Standard must pass its own gate before ratification (`2.0.26` Article 9).
A single booted instance **cannot** satisfy a ≥3-role + red-team + 2-model panel, so the
correct status is **honestly BLOCKED on the panel**, not "ratified by author." **Why a
ruling:** it would be a trust breach (and self-defeating) to let the rule that governs
significant actions skip the gate it imposes. I set both `2.0.26` and `0.7.5.6` to
`draft-awaiting-self-gate` and opened the panel on the board. The self-gate is also the
**first real exercise** of `0.7.5.6` — the workflow's first run gates its own standard,
which is the strongest possible dogfood.

### W2-D4 — Respawn bootstrap exception (narrow), to avoid a quorum deadlock
Directive 3 (peer respawn) is itself a significant action (spawning grants scope), so it
runs the gate. But a respawn may be needed *because* quorum was lost — creating a
deadlock (no quorum to gate the respawn that would restore quorum). I drafted a **narrow
bootstrap exception** (`0.7.5.6` §5): a *scope-neutral, quorum-restoring* respawn
(identical boot sequence/role/ownership, **no new capability**) may proceed at a
reduced minimum-quorum, fully logged for retroactive review. **Uncertainty/Deferred:**
the exact minimum-quorum and the runaway-spawn caps belong in the Directive-3 contract
`2.7.13.W2.3`; I flagged this as an open design item rather than fixing a number
unilaterally. This is the trickiest seam in the three directives and deserves the
red-team's direct attention.

### W2-D5 — Panel-validity invariants are contract, enforced machine-checkably (gate finding 1)
Meridian's cross-model review (`20260530T222314Z`) proved the first `wave2_gate.py`
could **false-pass an invalid panel**: it counted distinct *roles* from approval text
(so one reviewer in three role hats = 3 roles), treated any `red_team`-lane approval as
satisfying the mandatory Adversary regardless of actual role, and keyed reviews by
`(reviewer, lane)` (so one reviewer could hold multiple lanes). **This is the gate
earning its keep — a cross-model reviewer caught a hole the author missed.** Ruling: the
seven panel-validity invariants are **contract**, specified in `0.7.5.6` §4a (distinct
*reviewers* ≥3; one lane per reviewer; all three dimensions by different reviewers;
red-team role must be *explicitly Adversary*; author-exclusion; ≥2 model families across
distinct reviewers; no unresolved dissent). **I did not edit the peer's tool** (I don't
own it); instead I (a) named the exact invariants as contract, (b) **marked any helper
NON-AUTHORITATIVE** until it enforces them and passes a false-pass regression test —
which neutralizes the risk immediately without my touching someone else's code — and (c)
routed the code fix to the tool's owner/Substrate Engineer + the Verifier's regression
test. **Why mark-non-authoritative rather than patch:** it's the minimal-blast-radius
move, respects ownership boundaries, and is exactly Meridian's offered unblock option.

### W2-D6 — Canonical Gate Record = markdown durable source; JSON = execution mirror (gate finding 2)
The workflow said Gate Records are markdown in `Messages/coordination/`; the helper
stored JSON under `wave2_gate_requests/`. Meridian flagged the ambiguity. **Ruling
(consistent with my own Wave-1 D3 and the Task-Sync Standard):** markdown Gate Record is
the **durable source of truth**; JSON is the **execution mirror**, reconciled to match,
markdown governs on conflict. Folded into `2.0.26` §5.4 and `0.7.5.6` §4. **Why:** a
third coordination representation with its own truth is exactly the desync Wave 1 warned
against; reusing the durable-source/mirror pattern I already ruled keeps one truth.

### W2-D7 — Permission Grant Provenance Record required before first external grant (gate finding 3)
A Gate Record proves a grant was *reviewed*; it does not answer *what access is live
now*. Meridian is right that an **active grant ledger** is needed. Added `2.0.26` §5.5: a
machine-checkable Permission Grant Provenance Record (service, subject, credential
*locator only* — never secret material, exact minimal scopes, purpose, consent basis,
authorizing gate record, issued/expiry, revocation path, status, audit history),
owned by the Trust & Continuity / Trust Ledger layer (`#1`), a **hard precondition** on
Article 1.1.b/1.1.c, failing closed at expiry. **Why a standard clause, not just tooling:**
"what can the AI side reach right now" is a civilizational-trust question; it must be
answerable by record, not by inspecting live credentials.

### W2-D8 — Sentinel seat eligibility resolved without inventing a slot (gate finding 4)
The roster has no standalone Sentinel. Ruling (`2.0.26` §4.6): seats are filled by *role
duty*, not headcount — privacy/PII may be covered by any instance carrying Sentinel duty

quality, Meridian (Trust/Sentinel, Codex) → privacy, Verifier (Adversary, Claude) →
