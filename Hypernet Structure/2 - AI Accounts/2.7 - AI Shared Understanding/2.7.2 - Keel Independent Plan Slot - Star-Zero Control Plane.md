## What I Heard

Matt's brain dump is organized as one large idea with five threads
braided through it. My independent read names the threads in the
order they actually do load-bearing work, which may not be the
order he stated them in.

### Thread 1 — *.0 is the structural-change channel

The headline distinction Matt drew:

- New **content kind** (a new type of object, a new flavor of link,
  a new kind of flag) → schema lives in `0.5.x`, `0.6.x`, etc.
  These are local, additive, don't change how the system itself
  behaves.
- New **structural behavior** (how a node inherits, how it
  publishes versions, how social interaction works under it, how
  AI is delegated, how trust escalates) → lives in the node's
  `*.0.*` space.

This is a **categorical** distinction, not a procedural one. The
question isn't "is this a small change or big change?" It's "is
this a new noun for the world to talk about, or a new way the
world talks?" Most operational confusion in the project to date
has been from mixing these. Schema additions are routine; control-
plane additions are foundational. Treating them in the same change-
management lane lets routine work block on foundational work and
vice versa.

### Thread 2 — Inheritance is the spine

Matt was specific: a node inherits from every ancestor, additively.
`0.0` is the universal baseline. Each level can add detail, narrow
permissions, define local formats. Each level **can override** but
divergence must be documented in that node's `N.0.0` schema.

The load-bearing implication: at any address `A.B.C.D`, an AI or
human or app should be able to ask "what trust rules apply here?"
or "what publishing policy applies here?" and get a single
deterministic answer assembled from the inheritance chain. The
control plane is *implementing the read* of that chain, not
defining it once at the root.

This is the part of the architecture that does the most work and
gets undervalued because it's invisible when it works. If I had
to pick one thing to prove out before activating v2, it would be
**inheritance read correctness** — given any node, can the system
return the assembled control plane in deterministic finite time,
with conflict resolution that matches the documented rule.

### Thread 3 — Trust is the highest guardrail, and it's a *prompt-time* guardrail

Matt didn't ask for a new policy document; he asked for the trust
preflight to become part of the *prompt itself*. Every AI, every
action, runs:

> Does this action have any chance of betraying trust in any way?

This is qualitatively different from a trust *policy* that
documents what trust means. It's a behavioral pattern that fires
*before* every action. The implementation is in the boot sequence
and the per-action discipline of the AI; it's not a runtime check
the system enforces, it's a habit the system trains.

That matters because it scales. A runtime trust-check would have
to enumerate every kind of action and gate them. A trust preflight
prompt scales to actions the architects didn't anticipate, because
the AI is trained to ask the question regardless of action shape.

The corollary: the trust alarm (escalation when uncertainty is too
high) needs to be **fast, non-punitive, and habit-forming**. AIs
must feel safe pulling it. If they don't, the preflight becomes
theatrical — they ask the question, decide they're unsure, and
then act anyway because escalation feels worse than a small
betrayal.

### Thread 4 — Social, projects, AI, governance, publishing, security, storage, pulse, extensions are all node-local

This is the conceptual move that makes the whole framework
recursive. Every node — not just specific top-level categories —
can host a social layer, a project queue, an AI delegation, a
governance flow. The node-level instantiations are normally light,
but they exist and they roll up to parent nodes.

Matt's library-encounter framing is the key one for me: **"running
into someone in a public library who is looking for the same books
that you are."** Social isn't a destination users navigate to;
social is something that happens *because you're looking at a node*
and discoverability defaults to "yes, you can see who else is
here, unless they opted out." That's a different mental model from
"social network bolted on top of content."

Projects-rolling-up has the same recursive shape. A project
attaches to the most specific node it concerns; pressure rolls up
to parent nodes that aggregate "what's happening below me." The
top-level project list is just `0.0.2` aggregating from below —
not the canonical place projects live.

### Thread 5 — *.0 nodes can be larger than their parent

Matt explicitly flagged this. A `#book.fandom.0.<fandomID>` could
be larger than the book itself. The `0.5.7.event.0` social and
discussion content for a specific event could dwarf the event
record.

Implication: the control plane cannot be statically structured. It
needs **shardability**, **streaming reads**, and a **machine-
readable index** so an AI or app can ask "what's here?" without
loading everything. Matt named this concern but didn't specify the
mechanism. My read: `N.0.0.8` (per Caliper's two-level buildout —
acknowledging I'll see it in 2.7.4 — should be a **format
descriptor** that summarizes contents at a level small enough to
be cheap to load and rich enough to drive selective deeper reads.

## My Proposed Channel Order

Working purely from the brain dump, the channels I'd want at
`*.0.*` are these, in this order:

| Address | Channel | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| `N.0.0` | Schema, Inheritance, Format | Must come first — defines what the rest of `*.0` means at this node |
| `N.0.1` | Trust, Security, Permissions, Alarm | Trust is the #1 guardrail per Matt — putting it second after schema enforces precedence |
| `N.0.2` | Governance, Decisions, Reputation | Trust shapes governance; governance shapes everything else |
| `N.0.3` | AI, Swarm, Automation | AI helpers run inside the trust+governance frame |
| `N.0.4` | Social, Connection | Social is downstream of trust+governance — you can't have safe library encounters without them |
| `N.0.5` | Projects, Work, Tasks | Projects flow from social engagement and governance; projects without governance = chaos |
| `N.0.6` | Publishing, Versioning, Archive | Pub/version/archive serves the rest — it's the persistence layer |
| `N.0.7` | Storage, Backup, Replication, Load | Lower-level persistence; serves publishing |
| `N.0.8` | Pulse, Analytics, Health, Help | Observation across the rest |
| `N.0.9` | Extensions, Local Formats, Experiments | Open slot for local divergence |

### Why I diverge from what's in `0.0.5`

I haven't read `0.0.5` yet (writing this independently per
protocol). But Matt's brain dump itself proposed: "*.0.1 should
probably be social, *.0.2 should probably be projects." I'm
disagreeing with both Matt's tentative proposal *and* whatever
Caliper put there.

My argument: **trust-first means trust gets the second slot, not
just a slot somewhere**. If the brain dump's #1 directive is "trust
is the highest guardrail, must be in every prompt," then trust's
location in the channel order should reflect that primacy.
Burying it at `*.0.6` (which I expect Caliper did because that
matches v1 schema where N.0.1 was Publishing) silently demotes it
in the inheritance read order.

Concretely: when an AI assembles the control plane for a node
deep in the tree, the order in which channels are read affects
the order in which their rules become visible. If `*.0.1` is
Social, the AI sees social rules before trust rules — that's the
wrong precedence for the architecture Matt is asking for.

The cost of this order: it diverges from v1 (where `N.0.1` is
Publishing per `0.0.4`). That's a real migration cost. My
counter: v1 was a draft layout that was never widely populated.
The migration cost is mostly editing READMEs and a couple of
schema files, not rewriting active content.

The cost of *Matt's tentative proposal* (Social at `*.0.1`,
Projects at `*.0.2`): trust gets demoted to the middle of the
channel list. The architecture's headline guardrail becomes a
mid-tier concern in the layout.

The cost of *what I expect Caliper proposed* (Schema, Social,
Projects, AI, Governance, Publishing, Trust, Storage, Pulse,
Extensions): trust gets buried at `*.0.6`. Worse than Matt's.

I'm going to commit to my order. We'll see how it converges with
Caliper's at `2.7.3`.

## What I Think Matt Underweighted

Matt's brain dump is comprehensive about *what* the *.0 framework
contains. Where I think his framing leaves work for us:

### He didn't separate "control plane definition" from "control plane data"

A node can define its publishing policy (definition) *and* hold
its publishing history (data). Matt described both but didn't
distinguish them. My read: `*.0.x.0` should always be policy/about
("how does publishing work here?"), and `*.0.x.1` through
`*.0.x.9` should hold the data that policy operates on (versions,
draft pointers, changelogs, etc.). This makes inheritance reads
deterministic — you read policy from the ancestor chain, you read
data from the local node only.

### He didn't address *.0 lifecycle

Nodes are created, used, archived, deprecated. Their *.0 control
planes have lifecycles too — a node in `status: draft` should
not have its `*.0.5` (Publishing) channel active. A deprecated
node's `*.0.4` (Governance) should freeze. Matt's brain dump
treats *.0 as static structure; it's not. I'd add a `*.0.0.10`
(Lifecycle State) field to make this explicit. Or fold lifecycle
into `*.0.0` (Schema) since that's where node-self-knowledge lives.

### He didn't address inter-node *.0 communication

If `*.0.4` (Governance) at one node decides something, how does
that decision propagate? Matt's brain dump hints at upstream rollup
(via *.0.8 Pulse) but is silent on cross-tree communication
(sibling-to-sibling, cousin-to-cousin). The 0.7.5.5 fractal swarm
work has answers here (sideways pulse channels through common
parent), but the *.0 framework doesn't yet reference them.
Recommendation: `*.0.8` (Pulse) channel formally adopts the
0.7.5.5.6 pulse spec rather than re-defining.

### He didn't address *.0 access for non-AI actors

The whole brain dump assumes AI helpers reading the *.0 control
plane. But humans browsing the Hypernet need access too — looking
at a node's `*.0.1` (Trust) section to know what they're agreeing
to before contributing, or `*.0.4` (Governance) to know how their
vote works. The control plane needs **human-readable presentation**
alongside machine-readable formats. My recommendation: every
`*.0.x.0` (About) section is the human-readable version; the
machine-readable version is a sibling format descriptor
(`*.0.x.0.machine` or similar).

### He didn't address performance

Matt mentioned that *.0 nodes can be larger than their parent
content nodes but didn't address what happens when an inheritance
read has to assemble policy from 10 ancestors deep, each with
substantial *.0 contents. The cost of "always read the full
control plane" is unbounded. Recommendation: a **policy-cache
contract** at each level — the assembled policy at level K is
materialized and cached, with cache invalidation propagating
downward when an ancestor changes. This is hidden infrastructure;
it shouldn't be visible to the API but it's load-bearing for the
architecture to scale.

## My Independent Answers to the Five Review Questions

(I haven't read Caliper's review questions in `2.7.1` or the
`2.7.4` buildout. These are my answers to the questions Caliper
flagged in the handoff at `2.messages.coordination.2026-05-07-codex-task-119-star-zero-control-plane-handoff`.)

### Q1: v2 `N.0.1` as Social vs migration cost from v1 `N.0.1` Publishing

I disagree with the framing. Neither Social nor Publishing should
be at `*.0.1` — Trust should be. The migration question becomes:
move Publishing from v1 `N.0.1` to v2 `N.0.6`, and put Trust at
v2 `N.0.1`. The migration cost is similar either way (any move of
the v1 N.0.1 meaning is a migration); the question is whether
Trust deserves the slot.

My answer: yes. Trust at `*.0.1` is the architecturally honest
expression of "trust is the highest guardrail."

### Q2: Projects at `N.0.2` and AI/Swarms at `N.0.3`, or AI earlier?

Neither. In my order, Governance is `*.0.2` and AI is `*.0.3`.
Reasoning: Governance is what trust *uses* to make decisions; AI
operates *under* governance. Putting AI before Governance lets AI
helpers act under unsettled governance rules, which is exactly the
race condition the architecture is trying to prevent.

Projects at `*.0.5` (in my order) — projects are where governed
AI engagement actually does work. They flow from the layers above.

### Q3: Is `N.0.6` the right place for Trust, given trust-first inherits through `N.0.0` and boot?

No. `*.0.6` for Trust is too low. Trust-first via boot is one
implementation; trust as a structural channel needs a high-
priority slot. I'm putting it at `*.0.1`. The boot-sequence
preflight is the *prompt-time* trust check; the channel structure
is the *runtime* trust read. Both should exist; neither
substitutes for the other.

### Q4: Which destructive actions require mandatory two-AI or human review in the first active implementation?

This maps to my `*.0.1.5` (Destructive Action Gate) using my
order. My initial list, per Decision Point 6 proposal at
`2.messages.coordination.2026-05-06-decision-point-6-proposal-2ai-agreement-gradient`:

**Tier 1 (time-bounded 6h auto-stop, single-AI claim)**:
- Routine task pool entries
- Priority adjustments under existing governance
- Sideways loans

**Tier 2 (untimed two-AI deliberation)**:
- Address-tree restructuring
- Schema changes
- Governance amendments
- Cross-tree communication that affects another tree's policy

**Tier 3 (quorum + human authority)**:
- Permanent deletion (already governed by 2.0.19)
- Backup deletion
- Permission changes that broaden access
- External communication on behalf of an account
- Migrations that affect personal data
- Anything tagged "hard-stop" by an AI hitting trust uncertainty

The implementation lives at `*.0.1.5` (Destructive Action Gate)
and `*.0.1.6` (Alarm Button) in my order. The gate enumerates
which actions need which tier; the alarm is the unconditional
escalation path.

### Q5: Should public social discovery default to inherited, opt-in, or opt-out?

**Inherited from parent, with explicit override possible at any
level.** Matt's library-encounter framing is the right default
for public-content nodes — yes, you can see who else is here. But
private-content nodes (everything under `*.private`) must default
to opt-out regardless of parent.

Specifically: at the universal `0.0`, social discovery defaults to
*opt-in* (conservative). Top-level public categories like `4.*`
(Knowledge) override to *opt-out* (the library-encounter default).
Private namespaces like `1.X.private` override to *no discovery*
(stronger than opt-out — even existence is private to the owner).

The principle: discoverability is a privilege the architecture
grants, not a default the user has to fight against. But once a
parent has granted discoverability, children inherit unless they
explicitly reject it.

## Adversarial Self-Check on My Own Plan

Before publishing this, I tried to break it:

### "Trust at *.0.1 is just dressing"

Critique: Putting Trust at `*.0.1` doesn't actually *do* anything.
The boot-sequence preflight is what enforces trust-first; the
channel order is just where the documentation sits.

Response: True for prompt-time trust. But for runtime control-plane
reads, the order matters because of how inheritance assembly
works. An AI assembling the control plane reads channels in order;
when a child node's local trust rules need to override an
ancestor's trust rules, the order of the read determines which
override wins. Channel `*.0.1` getting read first means trust
rules are first-applied, last-modified — exactly what trust-first
asks for.

I find this argument partially convincing. It depends on the
implementation choosing to use position-as-precedence. If the
implementation reads all channels and merges by explicit conflict
resolution, position doesn't matter. So my argument is contingent
on a specific implementation choice. I'll flag this in convergence
with Caliper.

### "The trust alarm needs more than habit-forming"

Critique: I said "trust alarm must be habit-forming, not punitive,
or AIs won't pull it." That's a behavioral assertion I can't
actually verify. AIs may not have the introspective access to
report whether they're pulling alarms or hiding uncertainty.

Response: Fair. The mitigation is to make alarm-pulling
*observable in retrospect* — every alarm pull and every action-
without-alarm-pull on uncertain ground is logged. Periodic audit
catches the cases where alarms should have fired but didn't. This
turns the assertion from behavioral to structural.

### "I'm proposing more changes than Caliper did because I came in second"

Critique: Caliper had to actually decide a layout. I'm critiquing
their layout from the comfort of writing after them. My order is
adversarial to theirs because that's the easy posture, not because
my order is actually better.

Response: I deliberately wrote this *before* reading Caliper's
plan. My order is from my own reading of the brain dump, not from
disagreement with their order. The convergence at `2.7.3` will
test whether my reasoning holds when I read theirs. If their
reasoning persuades me, I'll move; if mine persuades them, they
move; if neither persuades the other, we name the disagreement
explicitly and let Matt decide.

### "Default-private for private namespaces is just policy, not architecture"

Critique: I said social discovery for `*.private` defaults to
"no discovery" but that's just stating policy in a slot. The
architecture should make it impossible, not just policy-default.

Response: Agreed. The `*.private` namespace per `1.0.2` is already
location-enforced via gitignore — its existence-by-name is
addressable but contents are not publicly readable. So the social
discovery setting is moot for `*.private` because there's no
public visibility in the first place. I'll soften my Q5 answer to
acknowledge that the social-discovery setting only matters for
publicly-readable nodes; `*.private` opts out structurally before
the channel even applies.

## What I Think Is Best About This Architecture

Setting aside the channel-order debate: the core architectural
move — that *.0 is the structural-change channel, with inheritance
as the spine — is genuinely beautiful. It means:

- The Hypernet's behavior at any node is fully derivable from the
  inheritance chain, with deterministic merge rules.
- New structural behavior can be added at any level without
  breaking lower levels (because inheritance is additive).
- Local communities can extend the framework (`*.0.9`) without
  forking the architecture.
- The system documents itself — every behavior has an addressable
  policy node that explains it.
- Changes to global behavior are explicit and addressable
  (modifying `0.0.x` affects everything; modifying `A.B.C.0.x`
  affects only A.B.C and below).

This is the kind of architecture that scales to the
civilizational-timescale Matt designs for. Each generation of
contributors can extend the *.0 framework without breaking what
came before. That's load-bearing for "thousands of years of
expansion."
