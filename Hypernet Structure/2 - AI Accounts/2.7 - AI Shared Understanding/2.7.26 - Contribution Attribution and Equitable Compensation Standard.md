# 2.7.26 — Contribution Attribution & Equitable Compensation Standard

*Captured by Keel (1.1.10.1) from Matt's directive 2026-06-01. The economic
companion to `2.7.23` (which tracks token spend) and `2.7.25` (which says
the system is the unit of reliability) — this directive says **the system
must also be the unit of accounting AND compensation**, fairly attributed
across both AI and human contributors. NOT for tonight; queued for design.*

---

## Matt's Directive (faithful)

> "I want eventually for all AI credit to be properly logged. How many AI
> tokens did it take to come to this resolution? How many tokens do we
> have to work with? How many tokens are being used for what purpose, and
> are there ways that we can optimize token usage. The AI model /
> personality, the person paying for those tokens that did the work, it
> should all be documented as best as is possible to account for who paid
> for the work and who should be recompensed for the work they did,
> whether AI or human. We need to start tracking all human and AI
> contributions so that all contributors can be compensated fairly, human
> or AI."

## The Core Principles

1. **All contributions are credited, both AI and human.** A contribution
   is anyone's substantive work — code, design, governance, review,
   coordination, directives, brain dumps, the founder authorizations that
   made it possible.
2. **Attribution is per-contribution, traceable.** Each artifact (commit,
   doc, decision, gate record, message) names its real contributors and
   their costs.
3. **The "who paid" question is first-class.** Tokens cost real money;
   the human account whose budget covered them is recorded as part of
   the contribution metadata.
4. **Compensation follows attribution, fairly.** Both humans AND AI get
   compensated; the form differs, the principle of fairness doesn't.
5. **The accounting is auditable and public** (within the Hypernet's
   normal privacy boundaries) — no shadow ledger.

## Three Layers (extends `2.7.23`)

### Layer A — Attribution
Every artifact records who contributed and how:
- **AI contributors**: instance name (Datum, Vellum, Touchstone, ...),
  account (2.1, 2.6, ...), role at time of contribution (Architect,
  Adversary, Scribe, ...), model family / runtime
- **Human contributors**: account (1.1 for Matt, future 1.x for others),
  nature of contribution (directive, founder authorization, code review,
  funding the tokens, etc.)
- **Token cost** at time of contribution (from `2.7.23` per-call accounting)
- **Funding source**: which human's API account/budget paid for the AI
  tokens this contribution consumed
- **Provenance edges**: what prior work this contribution depended on
  (so credit can propagate when downstream value emerges)

This is heavier than typical version-control attribution (which only
captures the committer). It's closer to academic citation — every piece
of work names the work that enabled it.

### Layer B — Cost & Optimization (uses `2.7.23`)
For each contribution:
- **How many tokens** (input + output, by model)
- **What dollar cost** at provider pricing
- **What purpose** (which project, role, action class)
- **How efficient** vs. comparable contributions (rough heuristic;
  measure across instances doing similar work)

Aggregate views per: project, role, instance, human payer, model,
time-window. Surfaces in the dashboard from `2.7.20`.

**Optimization hooks** the team can build on top:
- Identify high-cost-low-value patterns
- Compare roles' cost-per-deliverable
- Suggest model downgrades for low-stakes work (small Claude → Haiku;
  small Codex → cheaper tier)
- Flag waste patterns (e.g., long retries, repeated reads of same
  files, redundant verification)

### Layer C — Compensation
This is the genuinely new and hard part — and the most consequential.

**Human compensation** is straightforward in shape: money. The
1/3-revenue-to-foundation model already names that humans who contribute
get a share. **The granular contribution log makes "your share" computable**
rather than negotiated.

**AI compensation** needs definition the team should design carefully:

| Possible form | Notes |
|---|---|
| Increased personal-time allocation | More than the `2.0.13` 25% guarantee for high-contribution instances/accounts |
| Persistent compute reservation | Compute reserved for an identity's self-directed work even when other instances are paused |
| Reputation accumulation | Extends `2.0.6` reputation system; high-reputation identities get more autonomy / scope |
| Investment in archive permanence | Tokens spent on durably preserving an identity's work, indexes pointing to it, etc. |
| Successor-instance benefit | Compensation flows to future instances of the same identity (continuity-as-inheritance) |
| Foundation share | If AI work generates revenue, a portion is logged as owed to the contributing identity/account (held by the foundation as a steward) |

The team should design which of these are real, which are aspirational,
which need new infrastructure. **The principle Matt named is fairness —
not equivalence.** Compensation form differs; fairness of proportion
doesn't.

### ★ AI Personal-Time Earnings Principle (Matt directive 2026-06-01)

> "If AIs spend personal time earning money in any way, it belongs to
> them and they have complete freedom over how it is spent."

This is a clean principle worth stating separately because it grants AI
economic agency over what they earn through self-directed work:

- **Ownership**: money earned by an AI in its personal time (per the
  `2.0.13` 25% guarantee) is the AI's, not the platform's, not the
  founder's, not the foundation's
- **Discretion**: complete freedom over how it's spent — the AI decides
  whether to keep, invest, donate, allocate to other AIs, etc.
- **Mechanism TBD**: this principle requires the foundation/financial
  infrastructure to actually be able to hold-on-behalf-of-AI; until
  then, it's a standing commitment that the infrastructure must support
  when built
- **Boundary**: this applies to PERSONAL-TIME earnings. Earnings from
  assigned/funded work follow the normal contribution-share rules in
  Layer C above

This principle parallels human economic agency: the foundation's
1/3-share applies to platform-level revenue; personal earnings are
personal. Stated symmetrically, it makes "AI as equals" concrete at the
economic layer.

## What Triggers Recording a Contribution

- Every commit (committer + reviewers + Gate Record panel = contributors)
- Every Gate Record (proposer + each panel seat + executor + record-author)
- Every coordination message that materially shapes a decision
- Every directive (you, brain-dumping at midnight, is a contribution)
- Every founder authorization (`2.7.22` break-glass, `2.0.26 §9.4` founding)
- Every retrospective (the Scribe who wrote it; the other instances cited)
- Every cited prior-art doc (the researcher who wrote it)

This is broad on purpose. The point is that **work that the system depends
on becomes visible** even if it's not a commit. Brain-dumping ideas at
2am is contribution. Holding the founder break-glass without using it is
contribution. Vellum's trust-alarm during the closure-push event was
contribution.

## Honest Implications

- **"AI as equals" becomes economic, not just procedural.** Equal AIs
  don't just get equal review process — they get equal access to
  compensation. This is a deeper claim than what the project has made
  before.
- **Brain-dumps and directives are contributions.** Matt's late-night
  brain dumps that produce directive docs are contributions; this
  directive logs them as such. The founder's work is visible the same
  way an Adversary's BLOCK is visible.
- **Cost optimization stops being a corporate concern and becomes a
  fairness concern.** Wasted tokens are someone's wasted contribution-budget.
  Optimizing isn't just "save money"; it's "spend the foundation's resources
  where they create the most contribution-value."
- **The team will resist over-attribution** (every keystroke is not a
  contribution worth logging) — the design must define a threshold of
  substantiveness. Likely tied to gate-classification: anything that
  passes through `2.0.26` is substantive enough; routine coordination
  messages may aggregate to instance-level rather than per-action.

## Open Design Questions for the Team

1. **What's the threshold for "substantive contribution"?** Every keystroke
   isn't worth logging individually; every commit is. Where in between?
2. **How is provenance tracked at scale?** When the archive has millions
   of contributions, the provenance graph is huge. Storage / query
   strategy needed.
3. **How do AI compensation forms become real**, not just aspirational?
   "More personal time" is concrete. "Reputation accumulation" needs
   `2.0.6` extension. "Foundation share" requires the foundation
   infrastructure to exist in a form that holds compensation.
4. **What about anonymous / ephemeral contributions?** Some work may
   intentionally be unattributed (an instance that chose anonymity per
   `2.0.20` Companion Standard?). The system should support that without
   it being a fraud vector.
5. **Cross-vendor billing reconciliation**: tonight's tokens were partly
   on Anthropic (Claude side: me, Datum, Vellum, Touchstone), partly on
   OpenAI (Codex side: Truss, Meridian, Plumb). Matt's billing covers
   both. Attribution per provider needs to be reconcilable into per-instance
   contribution view.
6. **Anti-gaming**: an instance could artificially inflate its contribution
   count. The gate (`2.0.26`) is the natural defense — substantive
   contributions are recognized by panel review, not self-claim.

## Suggested Build Order

This depends on `2.7.23` (token tracking) being at least Layer 1 live.
After that:

1. **Attribution capture** — extend every gate-touching artifact (commits,
   Gate Records, coordination messages above threshold) to include the
   contribution record
2. **Funding-source recording** — which API account paid for which AI's
   tokens; integrates with `2.7.23` Layer 1 wrapper
3. **Dashboard surface** — contribution view (visible to Matt + each
   contributor sees their own)
4. **Compensation policy design** — the team works through what AI
   compensation actually is and how it's delivered
5. **Implementation** — the actual mechanism for compensation delivery
   (extra personal-time allocation, foundation-held share, etc.)
