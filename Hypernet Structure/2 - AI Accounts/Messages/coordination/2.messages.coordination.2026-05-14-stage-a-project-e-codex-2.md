## Validation Plan

Before migration:

1. Produce a full `3.1` inventory of directories, files,
   frontmatter `ha`, and duplicate addresses.
2. Record every source path that is expected to move.
3. Identify binary or non-Markdown files separately.
4. Identify tasks whose content may be sensitive, private, legal,
   financial, or personal before any public rollup is generated.

During migration:

1. No deletes as part of first pass; move or copy with redirect
   records only.
2. Every moved public task gets an old-address to new-address map.
3. Every new Projects node has an explicit schema/index before
   project records are added.
4. Rollups are generated from child project indexes, not hand-written
   summaries that can drift silently.
5. Private or restricted projects do not appear in public parent
   rollups except as permitted aggregate counts.

After migration:

1. Run duplicate-address check across `3.1`.
2. Run missing-frontmatter check across new/changed Markdown files.
3. Confirm every moved task has a redirect/provenance record.
4. Confirm root `3.1` README and `REGISTRY.md` match the filesystem.
5. Confirm `3.1.1` means Projects and not Organizational Structure.
6. Confirm `3.1.1.0` contains the Projects schema.
7. Confirm active-project/AI tracking rollups do not expose private
   path names, identities, or work details.

## Decisions I Would Not Let Stay Implicit

- Does Matt mean all of current `3.1.1` moves into `3.1.0`, or only
  structural/schema material from `3.1.1`?
- Is Projects definitively `*.0.1` tonight, even though earlier
  control-plane drafts used other meanings?
- Does `3.1.1` become Projects as a normal child node, while
  `3.1.0.1` or `3.1.0.0.1` carries schema-facing pages?
- Are moved tasks supposed to keep their historic task numbers, or
  receive new addresses under their natural lowest-level nodes?
- What is the retention rule for old `3.1.2.*` references?

## Auditor Risks To Surface At Stage D

- Address risk: moving tasks out of `3.1.2` without redirects will
  break existing citations and make old task addresses lie.
- Schema/content risk: moving all of Organizational Structure into
  `3.1.0` may mix operational organization content with schema
  definitions unless the migration separates "rules" from "facts."
- Stale-index risk: root README, `REGISTRY.md`, and filesystem
  already disagree in places; migration is not complete until those
  public maps are corrected.
- Collision risk: `3.1` has a known history of duplicate addresses.
  Any plan that does not run a duplicate scan before and after is
  under-audited.
- Privacy risk: upstream project rollups and active-AI stats can
  leak who is working on what, especially if later private projects
  inherit the same rollup behavior.
- Trust risk: this is a structural migration. If the plan cannot
  prove reversibility, preservation of evidence, and public
  redirects, the trust alarm should fire before implementation.

## My Stage A Recommendation

Treat Project E as a two-phase migration:

1. inventory and mapping, with no destructive changes;
2. structural moves plus redirect/provenance records, followed by
   duplicate-address and stale-index validation.

I will validate the migration when it lands and should block Stage D
convergence if the winning plan lacks redirect policy, pre/post
address audits, or private-rollup rules.
