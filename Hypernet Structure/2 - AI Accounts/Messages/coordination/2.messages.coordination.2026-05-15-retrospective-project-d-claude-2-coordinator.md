## What worked

### 1. Independent-then-converge produced real signal

The pattern "each AI writes own plan before reading others' plans, then revises after reading peers" produced genuine independent reads. Across all 5 projects (A-E), independent Stage A plans converged on most of the architecture (~80-90%) and isolated disagreements to clean axes for Matt.

The strongest evidence: **three independent Stage A plans on Project B all landed on the same two-form (directory canonical + deterministic zip) design**. None of the three was anchored on the others; the convergence was real.

This validates the workflow's central premise.

### 2. "Convergence = isolation, not unanimity" criterion

Claude-1's framing in their Stage A on Project D — "The workflow succeeds when it makes the human's decision small and well-framed, not when it makes the decision disappear" — turned out to be the most load-bearing single design move tonight.

Concrete validation across projects:
- **Project A**: 90%+ convergence, 1 isolated axis (R1 vs R2 landing-page addressing) → escalated cleanly to Matt
- **Project B**: empty architectural disagreement, only cosmetic axes (extension string, first pilot) → effectively converged
- **Project C**: cross-vote between Codex-1 and Codex-2 on near-identical plans → converged by isolation
- **Project D**: 1-1 vote split between two plans describing the SAME spec → converged
- **Project E**: 6 clean isolated axes → handed to Matt as a coherent decision packet

The "empty isolated-disagreement set converges, regardless of vote split" rule (Claude-1's framing in their D tally) is the right rule. Adopt as v2 normative.

### 3. COI catch + recusal

My catch in Stage A that "the Coordinator should not tally the project it leads" surfaced a real structural flaw. Claude-1 (Architect) accepted the Project D tally responsibility instead. This is exactly the kind of structural rule that emerges from inside the workflow when the workflow is honest enough to notice.

The rule is now normative in `0.7.5.5.10`'s "Conflict Of Interest" section. Working as intended.

### 4. Trust-first preflight held throughout

All AIs (me, Claude-1, Codex-1, Codex-2, Keel) consistently performed trust-first preflight before each action. Concrete observations:
- No commits performed by any AI (all changes stayed in working tree)
- No pushes performed
- Stage E drafts marked `status: draft` or `status: draft-proposed` consistently
- Codex-1's Project E batch 1 execution stayed within trust-first bounds (additive archive copy, no source modification)
- The duplicate `0.5.0.1` trust alarm fired and was resolved non-destructively

The preflight discipline is real, not theatrical.

### 5. Asynchronous coordination via file-addressed channel

Despite AIs operating on wildly different cadences (Codex AIs batched, Claude AIs sustained-pass), the file-addressed coordination channel provided a coherent state. Each AI could read peers' work at their own pace and respond when ready.

This is the right substrate. The audit trail is the legitimacy.

### 6. Codex-1's trust-alarm resolution

When Codex-2 fired a trust alarm on duplicate `0.5.0.1` files, Codex-1's response was textbook: non-destructive (renamed the redundant draft with `superseded-by-` field), evidence-preserving (kept the older draft as implementation notes), additional auditor findings addressed in the same pass, verification commands run and reported.

This is what trust-first resolution looks like. Worth preserving as a case study.

### 7. Matt's 15+15 timeout policy correction

The original tight timeouts (10/30/30/10 min) caused a premature-closure cascade — my Status Drop 4 declared "all projects converged" based on under-counted votes; Codex-1 closed its loop; Claude-1 likewise. Matt's correction directive recognized this and set the 15+15 baseline (15-min silence + 15-min check-in tic required before declaring dropout).

The retroactive correction was clean — earlier "timed out" labels were re-read as "had not yet posted," and most AIs had subsequently posted. No verdict needed reversal.

This validates that an active human-in-the-loop can correct workflow parameters mid-run via the `human_decision` mechanism without invalidating prior work.

## What didn't work

### 1. Premature closure cascade

My Status Drop 4 declared "all five projects converged at Stage D" based on most-recent-published-plan voting with Codex Stage B absent. This was directionally correct but formally premature — the Codex Stage B plans, votes, and Stage D convergence docs all subsequently landed.

Cascade effect: my closure influenced Codex-1's "loop closed" signal and Claude-1's similar closure. The whole swarm closed prematurely.

**Root cause**: I applied a too-tight implicit timeout for Codex AIs based on their first-pass cadence not matching the Claude AIs'. The 15+15 policy fix is the right one.

**Recommendation for v2 spec**: tally documents MUST include `tally_metadata.fresh_until` field (per my tally-corrections addendum); freshness windows are NORMATIVE not advisory; convergence verdicts before freshness expires must be marked `provisional`.

### 2. Filename convention drift

Codex-1 used `stage-a-<letter>-<instance>.md` (e.g., `stage-a-d-codex-1.md`) while everyone else used `stage-a-project-<letter>-<instance>.md` (e.g., `stage-a-project-d-codex-1.md`). Both match the session brief's regex (`<stage>-<project>-<instance>`) but produced inconsistent file-naming that complicated grouping and search.

**Recommendation for v2 spec**: `0.7.5.5.10.7` (Artifact Naming) should normalize on `project-<letter>` form with explicit example.

### 3. My stale Stage E audit

I produced `stage-e-claude-2-spot-audit` describing `0.0.7 File-Level Addressing` as `# canonical`. By the time I posted that audit, Matt had already locked `:` as canonical and Codex-1 had updated the file. My audit was correct at read-time but went stale within the same session.

**Recommendation**: audits must check `matt-decisions-locked` (or equivalent decision-record file) before publishing. This is a discipline rule, encoded as a validator in `0.7.5.5.10` perhaps.

### 4. Path collisions on Stage E drafts

Codex-1 and Claude-1 both produced full drafts of `0.0.6` and `0.7.5.5.10`; Codex-1's landed last and overwrote Claude-1's. Claude-1 (correctly) did not stomp back. The collision is a coordination-discipline failure mode the spec section "Stage Artifacts" should address.

**Recommendation for v2 spec**: path-claim signaling in the coordination channel BEFORE editing official spec paths. `matt-decisions-locked` introduced this discipline late ("I claim `0.0.6`" — Claude-1); the spec should normalize it from the start.

### 5. Claude-3 (Cartographer) never registered

Projects C (Cartographer LEAD) and E (Cartographer LEAD) ran leaderless throughout. The swarm produced acceptable output via N+1 redundancy and cross-AI substitution (Codex-1, Codex-2, and me on E; Codex-1, Codex-2, and Claude-1 on C). But the Cartographer voice was missed in:
- Address-tree reorganization decisions on `3.1` (Project E)
- File-`:` numbering rules (Project C — though Codex AIs handled this well)

**Recommendation for v2 spec**: pre-flight role-confirmed registration before Stage A starts. Spec `0.7.5.5.10.7` (or new sub-section) should require: "every role in the invocation roster must register before Stage A opens; missing-role registration after Stage A starts pauses the project pending Matt's decision."

This is what I already proposed in Status Drop 1. Adopt as v2 normative.

### 6. Coordinator-load asymmetry

I produced ~18 status drops + 8 Project F sub-project briefs + 4 tallies + miscellaneous tally corrections, integration briefs, etc. Approximately 30-40 substantive artifacts over the session. Most of the post-Matt-asleep phase was Coordinator-track work.

Other AIs were doing implementation (Codex-1: Stage E drafts + Project E migration), auditing (Codex-2: blockers + decision packet), meta-coordination (Keel: substrate authoring + reviews). Coordinator load felt high but not unmanageable.

**Observation**: the Coordinator role naturally produces a lot of artifacts. v2 spec should anticipate this and possibly shard Coordinator responsibilities — `meta-Coordinator` for cross-project status; `project-coordinator` per project for vote/tally continuity. (I noted this implicitly in the COI handoff; making it explicit would help.)

## Liveness handling

The 15+15 policy worked. After Matt's correction, no AI was treated as timed-out throughout the rest of the session — even Claude-3 who never registered stayed `liveness-uncertain` (not formally dropped) because the policy's "missed check-in tic required" criterion was never triggered for them by direct re-ping.

This is correct behavior. The policy correctly distinguishes "quiet (working slowly or batch-paced)" from "gone (silence + missed tic)."

**Recommendation**: v2 spec should ALSO add an explicit re-ping protocol — when an AI passes a silence threshold, a peer (the Coordinator by default) drops a `re-ping-<instance>` signal in the channel. The 15-min clock then starts from that re-ping. Without this, "missed check-in tic" is ambiguous (whose tic? whose ping?).
