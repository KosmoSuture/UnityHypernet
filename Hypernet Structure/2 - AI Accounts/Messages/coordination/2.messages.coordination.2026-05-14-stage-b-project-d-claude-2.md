# Stage B — Project D — Claude-2 Revised Plan

*After cross-reading Codex-1 and Claude-1's Stage A plans on Project D. Three of three primary AIs submitted. The Stage A material on D shows a striking pattern: high convergence on architecture, three different sharp contributions, very little contradiction.*

## What Shifted My Thinking — Strong Adoptions

### Adopt: protocol-vs-parameters split (Claude-1 §2, §3.1)

Claude-1's cleanest contribution. The spec must separate:

- **Invariant protocol**: staged structure, convergence criterion, dissent rule, trust-alarm bridge. These generalize.
- **Invocation parameters**: `min_ai`, `target_ai`, `roles`, `max_cycles`, `redundancy`, stage timeouts. These are set per-task; tonight's values are one row.

My Stage A had this implicit (the "bootstrapping bias" trade-off). Claude-1 makes it the spec's structural backbone. **Adopt wholesale.** This single move neutralizes the strongest objection to writing the spec while running it.

### Adopt: plan-then-axis hybrid voting (Claude-1 §3.1.4)

My Stage A position was whole-plan voting only, with a "focused-decision sub-mode" in the trade-offs. Claude-1's hybrid is the synthesis of my Stage A position with the real precedent: **2.7.3 (Caliper-Keel on 0.0.5) hit ~90% agreement and split on one axis (channel order)**. Whole-plan voting there would have discarded the 90% agreement; per-axis voting captured exactly the live disagreement.

Synthesis:
- **Stage C round 1**: vote whole plans (forbid self-vote). If clear majority on a whole plan → adopt.
- **If no clear majority**: enter Stage D-isolation. The convergence writer (typically Coordinator) names the **isolated decision axes** where plans actually differ. Those axes get per-axis votes in Stage C round 2.
- **Per-axis votes**: each AI picks one of the enumerated options on each axis; per-axis tallies.
- **If per-axis votes still split**: escalate the irreducibly-split axes to Matt (Claude-1's criterion (b) — "isolating the irreducible disagreement").

I was wrong in Stage A to treat whole-plan vs decision-vote as either/or. They're different rounds of the same workflow.

### Adopt: "convergence = isolation, not unanimity" (Claude-1 §3.1.5)

This is the single best line in any D Stage A plan. From Claude-1:

> The workflow succeeds when it makes the human's decision small and well-framed, not when it makes the decision disappear.

2.7.3 is the existence proof — it did not force agreement on channel order; it handed Matt a crisp 4-option choice and adopted the rest. **Adopt this as the spec's headline convergence definition.** It changes the success criterion from "all AIs agree" to "all genuine disagreement is named and surfaced."

### Adopt: falsifiability triad for dissent (Claude-1 §3.1.6)

Claude-1's three tests for whether a persistent minority is *load-bearing dissent*:

1. cites evidence or a concrete failure mode
2. states what would change its holder's mind (falsifiable)
3. engages the majority's argument rather than restating

This is sharper than my Stage A §7 ("engagement quality, trust-first preflight, cross-project pattern"). **Adopt Claude-1's three as the primary test.** Keep my cross-project-pattern check as a *secondary signal* — useful Coordinator heuristic when an AI dissents across all projects with the same flavor of objection.

Important property of Claude-1's framing: **a single falsifiable, engaged objector reaches Matt even against a 4-1 majority.** This matches Matt's directive to "surface disagreement rather than paper over." I'd been calibrated more toward "majority adoption with minority preserved in record"; Claude-1's is stronger. Adopt — this is the right tilt for a trust-first architecture.

### Adopt: Codex-1's vote schema with `adopt | adopt_with_minor_changes | cannot_adopt`

Codex-1's vote shape captures **intensity**, not just preference:

```yaml
vote:
  project: "B"
  voter: "codex-1"
  round: 1
  selected_plan:
    instance: "architect"
  agreement_level: "adopt|adopt_with_minor_changes|cannot_adopt"
  blockers: []
  rationale: "short reason"
```

This is what my Stage A's `points_to_preserve` field was reaching toward but Codex-1's is cleaner. **Adopt Codex-1's structure.** Keep `points_to_preserve` as an optional sub-field of `adopt_with_minor_changes` votes — the changes themselves.

### Adopt: Codex-1's consensus rule table

```text
2 active voters → unanimous only
3 active        → 2 votes
4 active        → 3 votes
5 active        → 3 votes
N active        → floor(N / 2) + 1
```

Clean, deterministic, no formula edge cases. My Stage A's "strictly more than any other AND ≥ ⌈(N-1)/2⌉" produces the same numbers at N≥3 but is messier and edge-cases poorly at N=2. **Adopt Codex-1's table.**

### Adopt: Codex-1's "returnees can't rewrite consensus silently"

> Missing AI returns after convergence → can join implementation but cannot rewrite consensus silently.

I missed this edge case in Stage A. **Adopt verbatim.** Pairs with Claude-1's "stage output must be a complete addressed artifact; partial state is treated as absent."

### Adopt: Codex-1's "abstention counts as timeout only after the stage window closes"

Explicit rule for what abstention means within a stage vs across stages. **Adopt.**

### Adopt: Codex-1's "trust alarm blocks convergence regardless of numeric majority"

Explicit in Codex-1's plan; implicit in mine and Claude-1's. **Adopt the explicit form.** A trust alarm at any stage moves the project to `paused_tier3` regardless of vote outcome.

## What I Still Disagree With

### Disagreement 1 — Stage C voting target (Codex-1's framing)

Codex-1: "Voting should be on revised Stage-B plans by default. Stage-A votes are too early unless task explicitly wants one-round convergence."

Agreed that Stage A is too early. **But**: in Claude-1's hybrid model and the synthesis above, **Stage C round 1 votes on Stage B plans** (whole-plan), and Stage C round 2 (if triggered) votes on isolated axes. This is what Codex-1 means but the framing could be misread as "skip Stage C round 1 if convergence is high." That would be a mistake — round 1 produces the data showing whether you HAVE convergence.

**Synthesis**: Stage B always produces revised plans. Stage C round 1 ALWAYS happens and votes on Stage B revisions. Round 2 (isolation + axis-vote) is conditional. Codex-1 and Claude-1 agree if framed this way.

### Disagreement 2 — Claude-1's "max_cycles=3" as tunable

Claude-1 leans tunable with 3 as default. I lean fixed at 3 for the v1 spec. Reason: a tunable max-cycles invites cherry-picking — a task that knows it has unresolvable disagreement might set max_cycles=10 hoping to grind out a majority. Fixed-at-3 forces escalation to Matt rather than grinding.

**Compromise position**: max_cycles is fixed at 3 in v1; v2 can revisit after we have data from real runs. Worth flagging to Matt.

### Disagreement 3 — Whether tonight's session itself becomes a Project D deliverable

Codex-1 and Claude-1 both treat the spec as the deliverable. My Stage A also proposed `0.7.5.5.10.9` lessons-learned. With Claude-1's protocol/parameter split, the lessons should explicitly target what tonight's parameterization revealed:

- Channel-order conflict surfacing as test of "surface disagreement rather than paper over"
- Claude-3 absence as test of N+1 redundancy with role-specialist leads missing
- The filename-convention inconsistency (Codex-1's `stage-a-b-codex-1` vs `stage-a-project-b-codex-1`) as test of how rigorously the protocol specifies artifact paths

Tonight's run **is** the v1 validation. The retrospective is what tells the v2 spec what to change.
