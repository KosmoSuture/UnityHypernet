## Granularity Operational Rule (from Matt this turn)

> You only go as deep as you need to for any particular node, but
> you try to be cognizant at all times of the schema and how any
> new additions should fit into the schema.

This is the answer to "granular-to-infinity vs Zeno-paradox" that
came up in the 2026-05-14 dump. **Stopping rule: depth is driven
by actual data, not by aspiration. The schema is the ceiling; the
data fills as much of the ceiling as it needs.**

## Immediate Decoded Threads

- **1.* needs a proper universal schema**: currently inconsistent
  across people; current Matt-1.1 is a guideline, not canonical.
- **Medical-as-major-channel**: not buried under Personal Data;
  deserves its own slot with deep schema for records, vitals,
  DNA, providers, insurance, conditions, emergency, research,
  real-time monitoring.
- **DNA addressing**: chromosomes/genes/SNPs get permanent
  Hypernet addresses for standardized cross-sample research.
- **Mandala-gated research grants**: per-segment DNA access via
  mandalas; researchers pay users for anonymous access.
- **Universal health-tracker standard**: device-agnostic schema
  so changing devices doesn't break the timeline.
- **Emergency break-glass**: medics can access pre-defined
  emergency-relevant subset under explicit protocol.
- **Real-time AI swarm during medical episodes**: user's swarm
  watches monitors, advises clinicians at no hospital cost.
- **Messaging needs 2-3 levels more depth**: ready for
  hundreds of thousands of imported messages across email,
  chat, SMS, social DM, calls, mail, forums.
- **Schema fleshed out**: minimum 3 levels; 5 preferred where
  data justifies it.

## Open Questions Surfaced

1. **Channel position of Medical**: currently buried under
   `1.1.6 Personal Data`. Should be its own top-level channel.
   Recommended: a dedicated medical slot (proposal: `1.X.6` or
   similar — see `1.0.4` for the proposed allocation).
2. **Mental-health and substance-use defaults**: extra-sensitive
   even within medical. Schema should default these to stricter
   gating than other medical categories.
3. **DNA re-identification risk**: even "anonymous" DNA can be
   re-identified when joined with other data (geneology
   databases, surnames, etc.). Schema needs explicit warnings
   and audit hooks.
4. **AI-swarm liability during medical monitoring**: not a
   schema question, but the schema needs to support the access
   pattern; legal architecture follows.
5. **Standardization vs flexibility**: Matt wants a common
   standard everyone can use. Schema must be opinionated enough
   to be useful, flexible enough to accept any data source.
6. **Migration of current Matt-1.1**: existing inconsistent slots
   (1.1.10 Embassy, 1.1.11 Profile, etc.) will need migration
   when canonical 1.* schema activates. Multi-day work.
