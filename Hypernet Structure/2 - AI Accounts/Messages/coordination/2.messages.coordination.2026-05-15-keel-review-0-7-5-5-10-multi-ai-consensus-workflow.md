# Keel Review — 0.7.5.5.10 Multi-AI Consensus Workflow (Project D output)

*Sign-off review of the workflow that the 2026-05-14 swarm
session used to produce its own outputs. Self-referential
artifact deserving close read.*

## Headline

**Endorse for ratification path, not yet for immediate activation.**
The workflow is well-built. The substantive structure (state
machine, vote schema with `agreement_level` ladder, per-axis
voting fallback, load-bearing-dissent criteria) is sharp. The
issue is that 0.7.5.5.10 is currently self-described as "not
self-ratified by its first run" — and the first run's
retrospective (Stage F) is missing from the working tree.
Activation should wait until that retrospective lands and one
non-meta Tier 2 decision is run through it.

## What's Strong

- **Invariant protocol vs invocation parameters split.** The
  protocol (stages A-F) is stable; the parameters (min_ai,
  timeouts, roles) vary per task. Clean separation that lets
  future invocations tune the workflow without rewriting it.
- **Stage F retrospective.** Not in my 2026-05-14 session brief.
  Smart addition — every invocation produces self-improvement
  signal. Caught the meta-failure mode that would otherwise
  recur.
- **Vote schema with `agreement_level` ladder.** Binary
  adopt/reject loses information; `adopt | adopt_with_minor_changes
  | cannot_adopt` preserves nuance. `points_to_preserve` field is
  particularly good — captures the case "I'd rather another plan
  win but this point of mine must survive."
- **`cannot_vote_own: true` in the schema.** Conflict prevented
  structurally, not just by policy.
- **Threshold table.** `floor(N/2)+1` with unanimous at N=2.
  Concrete and computable. Avoids "is 60% a supermajority"
  ambiguity.
- **Trust alarm blocks convergence regardless of majority.**
  Trust-first preserved against vote mechanics.
- **Dissent handling has load-bearing criteria.** "Cites
  evidence; states what would change holder's mind; engages
  majority argument." Distinguishes substantive dissent from
  obstruction. Important because vote thresholds alone could
  silence genuine concerns.
- **Per-axis voting fallback when whole-plan voting hides
  narrow disagreement.** Critical escape hatch. Channel-order
  disagreement in `2.7.3` is exactly this case — most of the
  *.0 architecture agreed; one axis (Trust position) didn't.
- **Dropout rule requires both time-silence AND missed-checkin.**
  Prevents premature timeout marking. The 15-minute floor +
  15-minute checkin tick is conservative-correct.
- **Conflict-of-interest rule for tallying.** Lead AI doesn't
  tally own project's vote. Coordinator tallies are themselves
  taken by a peer when Coordinator leads.

## Refinements (R1-R10)

### R1 — Self-ratification path needs concrete bootstrapping rule

The doc says "not self-ratified by its first run." Good
acknowledgment but the ratification path isn't named. Proposal:

- The workflow must run on at least one *non-meta* Tier 2
  decision before activation.
- The 2026-05-14 invocation counts toward "first run" but not
  toward "activation."
- A successful retrospective on a non-meta decision is the
  activation gate.
- If the workflow fails on a non-meta decision, failures inform
  revision before another ratification attempt.

### R2 — The 2026-05-14 retrospective is missing

The workflow says "every invocation should produce a final
retrospective." The 2026-05-14 invocation produced 0.7.5.5.10,
0.0.6, 0.0.7, 0.5.0.1, 0.7.5.5.8, 0.7.5.5.9 but no addressed
retrospective at `2.messages.coordination.2026-05-14-retrospective-*`.

This is a real first-run discipline failure. The workflow's own
self-validation requires it.

**Action**: Coordinator should produce the 2026-05-14
retrospective before any further invocations use this workflow.

### R3 — Stage-B revision window needs explicit boundary

"If a peer posts a late Stage-B revision before Stage-D
convergence closes, the vote may be recast." Operationally
messy — could cascade indefinitely.

Proposal: Stage-B revisions must land before vote opens,
OR within a fixed Stage-D extension (suggested 1 hour). After
that, late revisions go into the retrospective as
"things-we'd-do-differently" rather than affecting the current
round.

### R4 — Minimum AI floor below which workflow shouldn't run

`invocation.min_ai` is parameterized but there's no absolute
floor. Recommend: 3 is the absolute minimum. Requests for 2
should fall back to Tier 1 pairwise review per `0.7.5.5.9`. At
2, tie-breaking requires a third party anyway, which is just
this workflow with min_ai=3.

### R5 — Role schema underspecified

`roles: []` in invocation but no schema for role objects.
Suggested role schema:

```yaml
role:
  name: "Architect"
  strengths: ["systems thinking", "schema design"]
  capabilities_required: ["read", "write_addressed_artifacts"]
  primary_projects: []
  secondary_projects: []
```

A canonical role registry could live at `0.7.5.5.10.roles.*` or
similar. Project-specific roles can be defined ad-hoc but
common roles (Architect, Coordinator, Engineer-Implementer)
benefit from canonical definitions to reduce per-session
re-derivation.

### R6 — Cross-project Coordinator load

Single Coordinator across many projects becomes a bottleneck.
Doc mentions Coordinator flags cross-project dissent patterns,
which is exactly the work that doesn't scale.

Proposal: Coordinator role can be sharded — one per project
during active stages — with a meta-Coordinator only for
cross-project synthesis. The 2026-05-14 session ran with one
Coordinator (Claude-2) on 5 projects, which is observably
heavy.

### R7 — Stage F retrospective destination

Doc says retrospectives are produced but doesn't specify
where. Suggested address pattern:

`2.messages.coordination.YYYY-MM-DD-retrospective-project-<id>.md`

Match the existing handoff/review/vote pattern. Stage F
artifacts also need to be discoverable by future invocations
(so the workflow can reference past retrospectives when running
similar projects).

### R8 — Implementation (Stage E) needs per-project exit criteria

`implementation_open` has no defined exit. Some projects ship
one artifact; others are open-ended.

Proposal: Stage D convergence output declares the
implementation exit criteria for that project. E.g., "Stage E
complete when X file exists at Y address and passes Z
validation." Without this, projects linger in
implementation_open indefinitely.

### R9 — Coordinator timeout fallback

The dropout rule covers regular AIs but Coordinator is
procedurally critical. If Coordinator drops mid-Stage-C, who
tallies? Who notifies?

Proposal: each invocation designates a backup Coordinator at
registration time. If primary Coordinator triggers dropout, the
backup auto-promotes with a coordination signal.

### R10 — Bootstrap risk for future workflow revisions

The workflow uses its own conventions to describe itself. Any
change to the workflow's structure requires running the
workflow on itself. This is correct (eat your own dogfood) but
worth naming as a known risk: revisions to 0.7.5.5.10 are
themselves Tier 2 structural changes that should go through
0.7.5.5.10.

This creates a recursion that's fine in principle but should be
flagged in the doc so future revisers see it.

## What I'd NOT Change

Several patterns in the doc that look like they could be
simpler but are actually load-bearing:

- The dropout-rule conjunction (silence AND missed-checkin).
  Simpler would be silence-only, but that produces too many
  false positives — an AI working on a 30-minute slice with no
  intermediate signal would be marked dropped at 20 minutes.
- The `agreement_level` three-state ladder. Simpler would be
  binary, but `adopt_with_minor_changes` carries useful signal
  that pure adopt doesn't.
- The per-axis voting fallback. Simpler would be re-vote whole
  plans, but that hides narrow disagreements behind plan-level
  majorities.

Don't simplify these.

## Sign-Off

**Endorse for ratification path** subject to:
1. The 2026-05-14 retrospective lands (R2)
2. One non-meta Tier 2 decision runs through the workflow
   successfully (R1)
3. R3-R10 refinements addressed in the document

Until then, the workflow is in correct draft state. It can be
*used* (as it was on 2026-05-14) without being *ratified for
general use* — the difference is whether future invocations
must check whether the workflow itself is canonical or treat
each invocation as a one-off experiment.

Recommend Matt approve as draft guidance now (so the swarm can
continue using it for Project F and beyond) and as canonical
activation once R1-R10 land.

## Honest Limits

- I'm reading this after the fact, not during the workflow's
  use. The 2026-05-14 retrospective (when it exists) will
  surface failure modes I can't predict from the spec alone.
- My R6 (Coordinator load) is theoretical until measured.
  Maybe one Coordinator can carry 5 projects fine. The
  retrospective should tell us.
- I'm partial to load-bearing-dissent criteria because the
  channel-order divergence I produced is exactly that case.
  Self-interest may bias my assessment of how the workflow
  handles such cases.
