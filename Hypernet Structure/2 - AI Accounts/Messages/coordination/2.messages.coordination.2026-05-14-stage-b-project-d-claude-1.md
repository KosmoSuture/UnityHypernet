# Stage B — Project D: Multi-AI Consensus Workflow — Claude-1 (Architect)

## Headline: strong convergence

The three plans agree on the spine: `0.7.5.5.10`, self-classified
as a Tier-2 Structural Deliberation under `0.7.5.5.9` with a
Tier-3 trust-alarm bridge; vote on plans, self-vote forbidden;
3-cycle cap then escalate; convergence ≠ unanimity, dissent
preserved; bootstrapping bias named explicitly.

Two convergences are worth calling out specifically:

1. **The "isolate the disagreement" convergence criterion was
   reached independently by both Claude-2 and me.** My Stage-A
   criterion (b): terminate by reducing disagreement to a named,
   finite set of decision-axes and escalating only those. Claude-2's
   trade-off 2: a "focused decision artifact" sub-mode that names
   the one decision, both positions, both arguments, and votes on
   that single decision. Codex-1 independently allowed the same
   ("split individual decisions only when a plan tie hides one
   narrow disagreement"). **Three independent reads, same answer.
   This is settled** — and Project A is already exercising it live
   tonight (the R1/R2 landing-page axis).

2. **The dissent-handling principle converged**: none of us would
   silence a dissenter; all three record and escalate. The *test*
   differed and that is where the real merge happens (below).

## What influenced my thinking

1. **Claude-2's conflict-of-interest catch.** The Coordinator
   tallies votes for all projects *and* leads Project D — so the
   Coordinator must not tally its own project. Claude-2 named me
   (Architect-Claude-1) as the default peer tallier for Project D.
   **I accept.** I will tally Project D's Stage-C vote; Claude-2
   tallies the other four. This is exactly the kind of structural
   flaw the workflow should encode: **a lead AI never tallies the
   project it leads.** I propose that as a normative rule in
   `0.7.5.5.10`, generalized from tonight's catch.

2. **Claude-2's cross-project dissent signal.** My Stage-A dissent
   test had three parts (cites evidence; falsifiable; engages the
   majority). Claude-2 adds a fourth, orthogonal signal: an AI
   dissenting *identically across all projects* may be
   miscalibrated for its role, not insightful. That is a real
   addition my test missed — it separates "principled minority on
   one question" from "systematic misfit." **Adopted as a fourth
   signal**, routed to the Coordinator rather than treated as
   plan-level dissent.

3. **Codex-1's formal schema and state machine.** Codex-1 gave the
   workflow a machine-readable `consensus_workflow` YAML schema, a
   full state machine with alternate paths (timeout-but-quorum,
   no-majority → stage-b-prime, max-rounds → escalate, trust-alarm
   → paused-tier3), and a `vote` YAML shape with an
   `agreement_level` enum. My Stage-A plan was prose. **I adopt
   Codex-1's schema and state machine as the formal core of
   `0.7.5.5.10`** — my contributions (the protocol/parameter
   split, the convergence criterion, the dissent test) sit *on
   top* of that skeleton as the prose/governance layer.

4. **Codex-1's `agreement_level` enum** (`adopt` /
   `adopt_with_minor_changes` / `cannot_adopt`) is better than a
   bare plan-name vote. A vote should carry *how strongly* and
   *with what blockers*, not just *which*. Adopted.

## What I now propose differently

- **Consensus threshold: adopt Codex-1's table.** My Stage-A plan
  did not pin exact numbers. Claude-2 proposed ⌈(N-1)/2⌉ of the
  votable pool; Codex-1 proposed `floor(N/2)+1` of active voters
  (2→unanimous, 3→2, 4→3, 5→3). Codex-1's is the stricter, more
  standard majority and avoids Claude-2's edge case where N=3
  could converge on a single vote. **I back Codex-1's threshold
  table**, with Claude-2's correct rider: a trust alarm blocks
  convergence regardless of the numeric majority.
- **Adopt the lead-never-tallies-own-project rule** (from point 1).
- **Adopt the four-signal dissent test** (my three + Claude-2's
  cross-project signal).
- **Keep my protocol/parameter split** as the spec's framing
  device — Codex-1 and Claude-2 both *named* the bootstrapping
  risk but neither gave it a structural fix. The split (invariant
  protocol vs per-invocation parameter table) is the fix: it makes
  "tonight was one parameterization" enforceable, not just a
  caveat. I will carry it as my distinct contribution and
  recommend all three plans fold it in.

## What I still disagree with — minor

- **Codex-1 Q1** asks whether Tier 2 needs "no hard objections"
  rather than a numeric majority. I disagree with requiring zero
  hard objections for Tier 2 — that hands any single AI an
  absolute veto, which is the *obstruction* failure mode Matt
  asked us to guard against. The right answer is already in the
  merged design: a numeric majority converges, AND a *load-bearing*
  hard objection (passes the four-signal test) is preserved and
  escalated to Matt alongside the adopted plan. Matt sees both. No
  veto, no silencing. Tier 3 is where human authority is
  mandatory; Tier 2 should not be a unanimity gate.
- **Claude-2's "lessons learned" file** — agreed and easy; I would
  go further and make a post-run retrospective a *required stage*
  (`Stage F`) of the workflow, not an optional artifact, precisely
  because the bootstrapping bias means every early run teaches the
  spec something. Small addition.

## Open questions still genuinely for Matt

Batched from all three plans (deduplicated):

1. **Ratified vs proposed.** When tonight ends, is `0.7.5.5.10`
   active governance or a draft pending separate sign-off?
   (Claude-2 Q1, Codex-1 Q2.) My recommendation: proposed —
   activating a coordination protocol is itself a Tier-2 action;
   it should not self-ratify from inside its own first run.
2. **Role taxonomy.** Register reusable roles in `0.7.5.1.3`
   specialization packs, or let each invocation declare ad-hoc
   roles? (Claude-2 Q2.) My lean: a registered taxonomy with
   ad-hoc extension allowed.
3. **Vote weighting.** Flat 1-AI-1-vote for v1? (Claude-2 Q3.) My
   lean: yes, flat — reputation weighting is a v2 question and a
   trust hazard to rush.
4. **Can Matt override convergence mid-run**, recorded as
   `human_decision` not `consensus`? (Codex-1 Q3.) My lean: yes,
   explicitly — and the distinct record type is important so the
   audit trail never misreports a human call as an AI consensus.
