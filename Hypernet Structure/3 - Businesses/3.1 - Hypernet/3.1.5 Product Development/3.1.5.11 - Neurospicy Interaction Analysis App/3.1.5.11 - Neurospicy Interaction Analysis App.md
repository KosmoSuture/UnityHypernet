# 3.1.5.11 - Neurospicy Interaction Analysis App

## Purpose

Explore a side app that helps neurodivergent and socially reflective users review interactions using video, transcript, timing, gaze, pose, and body-language cues.

The product should be framed as a **social replay coach**, not a mind reader, lie detector, diagnostic tool, or emotional-certainty engine.

## Product Thesis

Many people want help understanding social interaction after the fact. AI can help by organizing observable cues:

- who spoke when;
- interruptions and turn-taking;
- visible gaze/head direction;
- facial landmarks/blendshape changes;
- posture and pose shifts;
- distance/orientation when visible;
- transcript sentiment and topic transitions;
- user notes about what they felt.

The system should generate hypotheses and coaching prompts, not definitive claims about what another person felt or intended.

## Hard Safety Boundary

The app must not say:

- "They were lying."
- "They were attracted to you."
- "They hated you."
- "Their feet prove they wanted to leave."
- "This microexpression means X."

Acceptable output shape:

```text
At 04:32, Alex looked away while the topic shifted to scheduling. This may be distraction, discomfort, thinking, or something unrelated. If this moment mattered to you, a low-pressure follow-up could be: "I noticed I may have rushed that part. Did the timing work for you?"
```

## Consent Model

Minimum v1 rule: do not record or analyze other identifiable people without clear consent, except for private self-review where local law and platform policy allow recording. Even then, default to consent-first design.

Recommended modes:

| Mode | Recording | Analysis | Sharing |
|------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Solo practice | user only | full local analysis | private |
| Consented session | all participants consent | session analysis | participant-controlled |
| Therapist/coach mode | explicit professional context | professional-guided | controlled by user and professional rules |
| Public video study | public/owned media | limited analysis | cite source, no sensitive claims |

## Signal Layers

| Layer | Data | Use |
|-------|------|-----|
| Transcript | speech-to-text, speaker turns | conversation structure |
| Timing | pauses, interruptions, overlap | interaction rhythm |
| Face landmarks | gaze/head pose, coarse expression features | visible cue timeline |
| Pose landmarks | shoulders/body orientation when visible | posture change timeline |
| Context notes | user-entered goals and feelings | personalized coaching |
| AI synthesis | observations + uncertainty | replay summary and practice prompts |

## Technical Feasibility

### Feasible Now

- Video upload or live recording.
- Speech transcription and speaker diarization.
- Face and pose landmark extraction.
- Timeline visualization.
- AI-generated replay summaries.
- User-owned private storage in a 1.* locker.

### Harder

- Reliable "microexpression" interpretation.
- Foot orientation unless the full body is visible and camera angle is good.
- Real-time multi-person social coaching without distraction.
- Cross-cultural and neurodivergence-aware interpretation.
- Legal/consent handling across states/countries.

## MVP

Phase 0 should avoid real-time judgment.

MVP:

1. User records or uploads a short interaction they are allowed to analyze.
2. App creates transcript and turn-taking timeline.
3. App extracts face/pose landmark events.
4. AI produces:
   - neutral observable timeline;
   - "moments to review";
   - possible interpretations with uncertainty;
   - suggested follow-up wording;
   - practice plan for next time.
5. Raw video stays local or in a private locker; derived features are encrypted and addressable.

## Trust Requirements

1. Raw video is private by default.
2. Analysis must show uncertainty.
3. Claims must be phrased as observations or hypotheses.
4. App should separate "what was visible" from "what might be true."
5. User can delete raw media and derived features.
6. No employer/school/partner surveillance mode.
7. No scoring other people without consent.
8. No medical diagnosis unless a future professional-grade product with proper approvals exists.
